Nissan Motor (7201 JP): back to an operating profit, still a JPY 39.7bn net loss

Interest paid rose JPY 13.1bn to JPY 63.4bn, and special losses and taxes took the rest

Nissan Motor reported first-half results on November 11, and operating profit was a profit of JPY 80.0bn.

A year earlier the same six months ran an operating loss of JPY 27.7bn, so the swing is JPY 107.7bn.

Even so, profit attributable to owners of parent was a loss of JPY 39.7bn.

What happens between an operating profit and a net loss?

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures — earnings releases, results presentations, half-year reports and annual securities reports on EDINET, Japan's filing system (the counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • Nissan's first half of FY2026 came in with operating profit of JPY 80.0bn, against a JPY 27.7bn operating loss a year earlier. It was the first first-half operating profit in two years.
  • Revenue was JPY 6,072.0bn, up 8.8%. JPY 110.0bn of cost cuts under Re:Nissan and foreign exchange supported it.
  • Interest paid was JPY 63.4bn, up JPY 13.1bn on a year earlier, while interest received fell JPY 10.6bn to JPY 27.0bn. The net interest burden is JPY 23.7bn heavier.
  • A special loss of JPY 27.4bn and income taxes of JPY 39.2bn also weighed, leaving a net loss of JPY 39.7bn. Holding the full-year forecast of a JPY 20.0bn profit requires JPY 59.7bn of profit in the second half.

1. What the JPY 80.0bn operating profit was made of

Nissan Motor is an automaker headquartered in Yokohama, Kanagawa Prefecture.

Company website: https://www.nissan-global.com/JP/

In the first half of FY2026, revenue was JPY 6,072.0bn, up 8.8% from a year earlier.

Global retail sales were 1.41m units, down 4.7%.

China fell 15.0% to 237,458 units, while the United States rose 5.5% to 469,215 units and Japan rose 6.3% to 197,227 units.

Sales rose even with fewer units because the average exchange rate was JPY 159 to the dollar, JPY 13 weaker than a year earlier, and because price increases and a higher share of upper grades progressed in the United States.

Operating profit was JPY 80.0bn, an operating margin of 1.3%.

A year earlier the operating loss was JPY 27.7bn, an operating margin of -0.5%.

The company explains the improvement as coming from foreign exchange movements and cost reduction in the automotive business, despite higher raw material prices.

Under the turnaround effort it calls Re:Nissan, it cut JPY 110.0bn of costs in the first half.

Since the start, the cumulative total is JPY 365.0bn.

It did, however, cut the full-year sales plan from 3.15m units to 2.97m to 3.00m units.

In China, demand is weak, the shift to new energy vehicles is faster than assumed, and competition is intense.

CEO Ivan Espinosa said the first-half results show that bright spots are beginning to appear, particularly in the main markets centred on the United States and Japan.

What the company gave as the reason for the operating profit improvement was not a recovery in unit sales but foreign exchange and cost cuts.

2. What the interest on JPY 9tn of debt has grown to

Non-operating income, the line below operating profit, was a loss of JPY 44.1bn.

A year earlier it was a loss of JPY 50.3bn, so it improved by JPY 6.2bn.

The improvement came mainly from derivative losses (losses on financial derivative transactions) falling from JPY 35.7bn to JPY 1.3bn.

Behind that, the interest balance got worse.

Interest paid was JPY 63.4bn, up JPY 13.1bn from JPY 50.3bn a year earlier.

Interest received was JPY 27.0bn, down JPY 10.6bn from JPY 37.6bn a year earlier.

The net burden is JPY 23.7bn heavier.

In its half-year report, Nissan lists rising interest rates among the factors behind the difficult business environment.

The debt balance also grew.

Interest-bearing debt, bonds and borrowings combined, rose from JPY 8,709.7bn at the end of March to JPY 9,056.7bn at the end of September, an increase of JPY 347.0bn.

That is a rise of 4.0%.

In the breakdown, short-term borrowings rose from JPY 118.2bn to JPY 153.9bn, up JPY 35.7bn, while bonds, including the portion due within one year, fell from JPY 307.9bn to JPY 276.1bn, down JPY 31.8bn.

Interest-bearing debt grew only 4% while interest paid rose 26%.

Rising interest rates appear to be at work.

Ordinary profit was JPY 36.0bn, which works out to JPY 44.1bn below operating profit.

Net cash in the automotive business was JPY 831.2bn. It is down JPY 339.2bn from the end of March, but cash on hand remains substantial.

Even so, the cost of more than JPY 9tn of debt has the power to erode an operating profit.

3. The distance from JPY 36.0bn of ordinary profit to a JPY 39.7bn net loss

Take ordinary profit of JPY 36.0bn and subtract the special loss of JPY 27.4bn, and pretax profit is JPY 8.6bn.

The largest special loss is a special retirement premium of JPY 20.2bn. Impairment losses were JPY 4.6bn.

Special gains came to JPY 16.7bn, including a JPY 12.8bn gain on the sale of fixed assets.

Up to this point the result is still a profit.

What erased that profit was JPY 39.2bn of income taxes.

A year earlier the same line was a JPY 2.6bn benefit, working in the direction of raising profit.

The company writes in its earnings release that it calculated the tax by applying the tax rate it expects for the full year to this JPY 8.6bn.

The materials reviewed for this post did not show the tax rate itself or a breakdown of the tax charge.

As a result, the interim net loss was JPY 30.6bn, and JPY 9.1bn was allocated to shareholders other than Nissan in its subsidiaries, leaving the JPY 39.7bn loss attributable to owners of parent.

The JPY 39.7bn loss cannot be explained by the rise in interest paid alone.

Against operating profit of JPY 80.0bn, the non-operating loss of JPY 44.1bn, the special loss of JPY 27.4bn and the income taxes of JPY 39.2bn each sit between a third and a little over half of the operating profit.

Within non-operating items, interest paid of JPY 63.4bn is the largest expense line.

The JPY 39.2bn tax charge is also a heavy burden.

The structure is this: operating profit is only JPY 80.0bn, so it cannot carry the charges on the lines below it.

4. What the JPY 20.0bn full-year profit forecast asks of the second half

The company did not change its full-year forecast.

Operating profit is put at JPY 200.0bn, and net income attributable to owners of parent at JPY 20.0bn.

The first-half net loss was JPY 39.7bn, so turning that into a JPY 20.0bn profit for the full year requires JPY 59.7bn of profit in the second half.

The second-half operating profit forecast is JPY 120.0bn.

The company expects higher unit sales, new model launches and an improved product mix to pull profit up.

Along with progress on Re:Nissan, it expects an effect that outweighs the negatives from foreign exchange and raw material costs and the negative from the temporary tariff benefit booked in the first half, which will not repeat.

How much of the JPY 120.0bn operating profit remains in the bottom line depends on the size of interest and taxes.

On simple multiplication, a 0.1 percentage point rise in the interest rate on JPY 9,056.7bn of interest-bearing debt adds about JPY 9.0bn of cost a year.

The company plans to announce a new business plan in February 2027.

Why it matters for global investors

Japanese companies mostly close their financial year on March 31, so the year Nissan calls FY2026 runs from April 2026 to March 2027, and these results cover April to September 2026. The company publishes only a full-year forecast, which means the second-half figure quoted above is derived by subtracting the first half from the plan — an inference from the company's numbers, not a number the company disclosed. The underlying documents here, the earnings release, the results presentation and the half-year report, are published in Japanese, and EDINET, the Financial Services Agency's disclosure system, is where the filings themselves sit.

Closing

The return to an operating profit is the result of foreign exchange and cost cuts working.

That JPY 80.0bn, though, is still thin in front of the interest on JPY 9tn of debt, the special losses and the taxes.

What the first-half numbers show is that even with operating profit back in the black, the interest and tax burden is heavy enough to keep it out of the bottom line.
