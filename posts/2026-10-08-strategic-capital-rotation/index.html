Strategic Capital cut four stakes and raised A&D HOLON to 16.72%

Reading the direction of change in Japan's large shareholding reports

Ask about an "activist shareholder" in Japan and the image that comes to mind is an investor buying up shares and pressing management for change.

But read large shareholding reports in sequence, and it turns out that very few investors are only buying.

This week's filings offered a clear example: the investment firm Strategic Capital, Inc.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • In 2026, Strategic Capital has kept reducing its holding ratios in four companies: Noritake, Keihanshin Building, YODOKO and Yellow Hat.
  • Over the same period, one holding went the other way: A&D HOLON Holdings, from 5.24% to 16.72%.
  • In the four sold positions, the reported disposals were made at prices above EDINET DB's estimated acquisition prices.
  • In other words, this investor is not only accumulating. It is rotating from one name to another. The direction of change in the filings says more about what is happening than the "activist" label does.

1. The "activist" label does not only mean a buyer

What Strategic Capital is

Strategic Capital is a company that invests in listed Japanese companies. It was founded in 2012 by people who came from the former Murakami Fund.

It is known for investing in companies whose corporate value it judges not to be reflected in the share price, and for engaging with them as a shareholder to push for improvement.

Company website: https://stracap.jp/

How the filings treat it

EDINET DB classifies Strategic Capital's purpose of holding as "activist" for all five positions covered in this article.

A large shareholding report is a document filed by any shareholder that holds more than 5% of a company's shares outstanding.

After that, an amendment report is filed each time the ratio moves by 1% or more.

Stack those filings together and the history of increases and decreases is preserved as it happened.

The label is the same "activist" in every case, but the history mixes selling and buying.

2. In four names, the holding ratio has fallen since the start of the year

Start with the selling side.

Noritake (5331)

Noritake makes tableware, and also grinding tools and electronic materials that draw on its ceramics technology.

Company website: https://www.noritake.co.jp/

The holding ratio fell from 9.38% on January 30, 2026 to 4.36% on June 4.

The filing date shown on EDINET DB is October 7.

Keihanshin Building (8818)

Keihanshin Building is an Osaka-based real estate company that leases office buildings and data center buildings, among other properties.

Company website: https://www.keihanshin.co.jp/

The ratio went from 11.99% on April 22 to 8.02% on September 17.

The report was filed on September 29.

YODOKO (5451)

YODOKO, formerly Yodogawa Steel Works, makes steel sheet products such as color-coated steel sheet, as well as building materials and consumer goods such as outdoor storage sheds.

Company website: https://www.yodoko.co.jp/

The ratio fell from 7.84% on February 2 to 4.57% on September 28.

Because it dropped below 5%, this filing took the position out of the large shareholding report regime.

The report was filed on October 5.

Yellow Hat (9882)

Yellow Hat operates car accessories stores across Japan.

The ratio went from 13.83% on January 13 to 11.73% on September 25.

The decline is smaller, but it is the second consecutive drop of 1% or more, following the report of July 31.

The report was filed on October 2.

Across the four names, the common thread is a run of filings, from the start of the year to the latest, each showing a step down of 1% or more.

3. The sale prices are above the estimated acquisition price

So at what prices were the shares sold?

EDINET DB calculates an estimated acquisition price from each filing.

The table below sets those estimates next to the weighted average price of the disposals for which the latest report states a price.

All four disposals came at 1.4x to 1.7x the estimated acquisition price.

That comparison comes with caveats.

The estimated acquisition price is a figure calculated by EDINET DB, not a number disclosed by the company.

The disposal price is also an average only over the shares for which the report states a price.

In YODOKO's September 28 report, for example, of the 2,075,900 shares disposed, a price is stated for 857,100 of them.

So this does not prove that every sale was profitable.

What it shows is narrower: within the disclosed range, the selling was done above the estimated acquisition price.

4. A&D HOLON is the one position being bought

If the story were only selling, it would be a simple retreat.

But one holding moved in the opposite direction over the same period.

A&D HOLON Holdings (7745)

A&D HOLON Holdings manufactures and sells semiconductor-related equipment, measuring and weighing instruments, and medical and health equipment.

Strategic Capital's holding ratio rose from 5.24% on February 5 to 16.72% on August 27, across 11 filings.

Each report gives a reason of an increase of 1% or more, or a change such as a pledge agreement.

The timing overlaps with the four sales.

Whether the proceeds from those sales went into buying A&D HOLON, however, cannot be seen from the disclosures.

No document showing the flow of funds was in the scope checked for this article.

What is certain is that positions being reduced and positions being increased exist at the same time within the same investor.

5. When reading a filing, look at the direction of change, not the label

Here is the sequence in summary.

The "activist" classification does not tell you whether that investor is currently buying or selling a given stock.

Four positions are down and one is up: the direction differs even within the same company's filings.

So when you look at a large shareholding report, two checks are the shortcut.

The first is whether the filing in front of you is an increase or a decrease.

The second is whether that direction has continued when the past reports are lined up.

A search tool such as EDINET DB lets you list the history by specifying the filer and the issuer.

This is not a forecast about any individual company's future.

It is only about how to read facts that have been disclosed.

Why it matters for global investors

In Japan, any investor that crosses 5% of a listed company's shares must file a large shareholding report, and every further move of 1% or more requires an amendment report, so the filings leave a dated trail of direction rather than a one-off label.

These reports are filed through EDINET, the Financial Services Agency's disclosure system, and are published in Japanese, which is one reason the sequence of increases and decreases is easy to miss from outside Japan.

A holder classified as an activist can be trimming one position while building another at the same time, as the four reductions and the A&D HOLON build-up here show, so the direction of change is the part worth checking first.

Closing

Headlines about activist filings tend to read like buying stories.

Open the history, though, and there are days of buying and days of quietly trimming.

Next time a filing catches your eye for a name you follow, put the change figure next to the one before it.

That alone should make the same document look a little different.
