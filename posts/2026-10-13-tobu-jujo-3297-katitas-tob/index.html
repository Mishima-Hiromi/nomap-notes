Tobu Jujo (3297): what sits behind a 72.56% tender offer premium

A JPY 2,000 offer, the book value it is measured against, and four rounds of negotiation

In tender offer news, the big number is the first thing you see: a premium of 72.56%.

What that 72.56% is measured against is rarely explained.

Here is what the tender offer for Tobu Jujo (3297), announced on October 13, looks like in the company's own disclosure.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • Katitas (8919), which refurbishes and resells pre-owned homes, will run a tender offer for Tobu Jujo at JPY 2,000 per share to make it a wholly owned subsidiary.
  • JPY 2,000 is 72.56% above the October 9 close of JPY 1,159.
  • But Tobu Jujo's shares had been trading about 33% below book value per share of JPY 1,741.88. The offer is about 1.15x book value.
  • The special committee pushed back four times, starting from the first proposal of JPY 1,700, before agreeing at JPY 2,000.
  • Three holders led by the president, with 45.01% ownership, have already signed tender agreements.

1. The buyer and the target

Katitas (8919)

Katitas is headquartered in Kiryu, Gunma Prefecture, and refurbishes pre-owned homes.

It buys used homes itself, renovates them, and sells them.

It is listed on the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange. Its largest shareholder is Nitori Holdings (34.14% as of March 31, 2026).

Tobu Jujo (3297)

Tobu Jujo is a real estate company headquartered in Shimonoseki, Yamaguchi Prefecture.

It buys homes 20 to 40 years old and resells them, and also brokers real estate sales and rentals.

It is listed on the Standard Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange and on the Fukuoka Stock Exchange Q-Board.

For the fiscal year ended May 2026, revenue was JPY 7.75bn, operating profit was JPY 0.59bn, and net income was JPY 0.40bn.

Earnings per share (EPS) were JPY 145.85, and the dividend per share was JPY 43.

The tender offer is between two companies whose businesses are close to each other.

2. What the 72.56% is measured against

Katitas is offering JPY 2,000 per common share.

The offer period runs for 35 business days, from October 14 to December 3, 2026.

The JPY 2,000 price is a 72.56% premium to the close of JPY 1,159 on the last business day before the announcement (October 9).

Against the simple average closing price over the past month (JPY 1,179), the premium is 69.64%.

So 72.56% is measured against the recent share price.

The next question is whether that recent share price was high relative to what the company owns.

Tobu Jujo's book value per share was JPY 1,741.88 at the end of May 2026.

Divide the October 9 close of JPY 1,159 by that, and you get about 0.67x.

Divide JPY 2,000 by it, and you get about 1.15x.

The share price stood about 33% below the value on the books, and the tender offer price roughly closes that gap and goes a little beyond it.

Divide JPY 2,000 by EPS of JPY 145.85, and the price-to-earnings ratio (PER) is about 13.7x.

At the October 9 close, it was about 7.9x.

Set side by side, the large 72.56% figure is also the flip side of how low the original share price was.

3. How the price was set

According to Tobu Jujo's disclosure, price negotiations began on September 16.

Katitas's first proposal was JPY 1,700.

On the following day, September 17, the special committee (a committee set up independently of the company to protect the interests of general shareholders) asked for a new proposal.

Its reason was that the price did not reflect the company's intrinsic value and was far from the price level the committee had in mind.

The sequence that followed was as follows.

  • September 28: JPY 1,800 proposed. The special committee requested a new proposal the same day.
  • October 1: JPY 1,900 proposed. A new proposal was requested on October 2.
  • October 6: JPY 2,000 proposed. On October 8, the special committee asked for JPY 2,090.
  • October 9: Katitas said it could not raise the price any further and made JPY 2,000 its final proposal. The special committee accepted.

The price was raised three times from JPY 1,700, and at one point the special committee asked for JPY 2,090.

Tobu Jujo's third-party valuation firm (AGS FAS) put the value of one share at the following levels.

Market price method: JPY 1,159 to JPY 1,196

DCF method: JPY 1,819 to JPY 2,487

--- From Tobu Jujo's notice of opinion in support and recommendation to tender

JPY 2,000 is above the upper end of the market price method range and inside the DCF (discounted cash flow) range.

Tobu Jujo cites this as one of the reasons for its support.

On the Katitas side, the third-party valuation firm (Daiwa Institute of Research) has a DCF range of JPY 1,306 to JPY 2,745, wider than the range on the Tobu Jujo side.

JPY 2,000 falls inside both ranges.

Whether the price is appropriate, however, is for each shareholder to decide when deciding whether to tender.

4. 45% has already promised to tender

There is more to this deal than the price.

Tobu Jujo's president and largest shareholder, Toshihiro Ogino (38.66%), his asset management company OTC Corporation (5.11%), and his spouse Shitomi Ogino (1.24%) have signed tender agreements with Katitas.

Together the three hold 45.01%.

For the tender offer to succeed, at least 1,776,600 shares (65.56% ownership) must be tendered.

Even after subtracting the three holders, about 20% of the shares still have to be tendered by the remaining shareholders for the offer to clear that minimum.

According to the disclosure, the seller moved first.

In late April 2026, Ogino told Katitas that he intended to transfer the shares he owned.

There is one more decision that affects shareholders.

Tobu Jujo has resolved that, conditional on the tender offer succeeding, it will not pay a year-end dividend for the fiscal year ending May 2027 and will abolish its shareholder benefit program.

The dividend per share for the fiscal year ended May 2026 was JPY 43.

Katitas's proposal also assumes that no year-end dividend will be paid.

Shareholders who do not tender are expected to be squeezed out later, in a procedure that removes minority shareholders and acquires all remaining shares.

The disclosure says the consideration in that case is expected to be calculated to match the tender offer price.

The listing is scheduled to be discontinued.

Why it matters for global investors

Tobu Jujo is listed on the Standard Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange and on the Fukuoka Stock Exchange Q-Board, a regional exchange, and its disclosure is published in Japanese. The company is controlled by its founding family: the president, his asset management company and his spouse hold 45.01%, and they had already agreed to tender before minority shareholders had to decide. That is why the remaining shareholders matter for whether the offer reaches its 65.56% minimum, and why the special committee's negotiation is the part of the process that speaks for them. Tender offer documents and valuation reports in Japan are usually available only in Japanese, so the premium in the headline is often the only part that reaches readers outside the country.

Closing

The meaning of a "premium" in a tender offer headline changes once you check what it is measured against.

Here, the original share price was only about two-thirds of book value, and that is what produced such a large premium.

A shareholder who wanted to sell, a committee that held out, and a company that wanted to buy.

Look at the JPY 2,000 they settled on by following where each number came from.
