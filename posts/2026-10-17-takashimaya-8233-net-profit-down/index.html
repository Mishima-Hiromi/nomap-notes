Takashimaya (8233 JP): operating profit up 16.4%, net income down 24.5%

The two numbers parted company below ordinary profit, not in the stores

A 24.5% fall in net income sounds like shoppers stopped spending.

That is not what Takashimaya's first-half results, published on October 15, showed.

Core profit rose, and the company raised its full-year operating profit guidance.

Here is where the figures parted company, step by step.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from Japanese-language earnings releases to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • For the six months from March to August 2026, the first half of the fiscal year ending February 2027, operating profit rose 16.4% to JPY 27.5bn.
  • Net income for the same half fell 24.5% to JPY 16.0bn.
  • The divergence sits below operating profit: the gap between ordinary profit and net income widened from JPY 0.8bn a year earlier to JPY 12.1bn.
  • Full-year operating profit guidance was raised from JPY 57.5bn to JPY 59.0bn, while net income guidance stayed at JPY 38.0bn.

1. Revenue up 3.7%, with operating and ordinary profit both higher

What Takashimaya is

Takashimaya Company, Limited runs department stores under the Takashimaya name.

It also develops and operates department stores in Japan and overseas, along with commercial facilities such as shopping centers.

Its shares carry securities code 8233.

Company website: https://www.takashimaya.co.jp/

First-half consolidated results

The table sets the first half of the fiscal year ending February 2027 beside the same period a year earlier.

Revenue rose from JPY 235.4bn to JPY 244.1bn, up 3.7%.

Operating profit, the profit the core business earns, rose from JPY 23.7bn to JPY 27.5bn, up 16.4%.

Ordinary profit, operating profit plus items such as interest and dividend income, also rose 27.5%, to JPY 28.1bn.

Up to this point, every line is higher.

Only net income fell, from JPY 21.2bn to JPY 16.0bn.

2. The figures diverged below ordinary profit

Net income is set from ordinary profit after adjusting for extraordinary items and taxes.

That adjustment is measured here as the difference between ordinary profit and net income for the half.

A year earlier the gap was only JPY 0.8bn.

This time it is JPY 12.1bn.

Ordinary profit rose by JPY 6.0bn, yet net income fell by JPY 5.2bn.

The breakdown of that gap, meaning what sits inside the extraordinary items and the tax charge, could not be read from the materials available this time.

So I will not conclude what caused it.

What can be said is where it happened: items below ordinary profit canceled out the gains in operating and ordinary profit.

Reading the headline "net income down 24.5%" as a sign that the department store business itself weakened would be premature.

3. Department stores at home and abroad carried the core business

The segment figures show what drove the rise in operating profit.

The largest contributor is the domestic department store business.

Revenue rose 1.9% to JPY 149.1bn, while operating profit rose 30.2% to JPY 12.5bn.

Profit grew far faster than revenue.

The overseas department store business reported revenue of JPY 18.6bn, up 15.8%, and operating profit of JPY 4.6bn, up 21.4%.

The overseas commercial development business lifted operating profit 19.2% to JPY 3.2bn, and the finance business 17.0% to JPY 3.1bn.

The segments that fell were store interiors, the business that fits out shops, down 30.7% to JPY 1.1bn, and other businesses, down 20.3% to JPY 0.6bn.

Segment operating profit is stated before internal transaction adjustments.

The segments therefore do not add up to the consolidated operating profit of JPY 27.5bn.

Even so, the department stores in Japan and overseas are the businesses that lifted profit.

4. The company raised its core outlook and left net income alone

On the same day, the company updated its full-year forecasts.

Full-year operating profit guidance was raised by JPY 1.5bn, from JPY 57.5bn to JPY 59.0bn.

Against prior-year operating profit of JPY 53.5bn, that is guidance for 10.2% growth.

The first-half figure of JPY 27.5bn equals 46.6% of the new full-year guidance.

A year earlier, the first half had been 44.2% of that year's full-year operating profit.

This year is therefore running slightly ahead of last year's pace.

Full-year net income guidance, by contrast, stays at JPY 38.0bn.

First-half net income of JPY 16.0bn is 42.1% of that.

The annual dividend per share was raised from JPY 40 to JPY 44.

A higher core outlook, an unchanged net income outlook, and a larger dividend.

That is the answer the company gave this time.

In other words, this fall in net income reads less like a signal that the core business has deteriorated and more like a dip that appears in the year-on-year comparison.

The cause of that dip, though, needs to be confirmed in the company's own earnings release.

Why it matters for global investors

Ordinary profit is a Japanese-GAAP stage that sits between operating profit and net income, and it does not exist under IFRS or US GAAP, so readers outside Japan often have to map it onto their own framework before comparing. The gap from ordinary profit down to net income is where one-off gains and losses and the tax charge sit, which is why a headline net income figure can move against the operating business. Takashimaya's fiscal year also ends in February, so its first half runs from March to August rather than from January to June. Segment figures in Japanese filings are usually stated before internal adjustments, as here, and the filings themselves are published in Japanese, so reconcile against the consolidated line and check the originals.

Closing

Headline numbers that move in the same direction can still come from different places.

At Takashimaya, the divergence came below ordinary profit.

Rather than judging by the headline decline alone, it is worth going back to the point where the numbers separated.
