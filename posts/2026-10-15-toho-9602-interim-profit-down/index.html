TOHO (9602 JP): operating profit down 24.8% as costs rose 19.8%

Revenue barely moved; the hit to profit came from expenses, not from the box office

A 24.8% fall in operating profit sounds like the films did not sell.

TOHO's first-half results, published on October 15, showed something different.

Revenue barely moved. Something else took the profit down.

Here is the arithmetic, step by step.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from Japanese-language earnings releases to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • For the first half (March to August) of the fiscal year ending February 2027, operating profit fell 24.8% to JPY 30.9bn.
  • About 84% of that decline is explained not by lower gross profit but by higher selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses.
  • Full-year operating profit guidance of JPY 62.0bn is unchanged. That assumes the second half earns about JPY 4.4bn more than the prior-year second half.
  • The only figure raised was net income, from JPY 41.0bn to JPY 48.0bn, and the reason is a sale of policy-shareholdings. The core outlook did not move.

1. Operating profit fell about JPY 10.2bn, and most of it is cost

What TOHO is

TOHO produces and distributes films, runs the TOHO Cinemas chain, and is also in real estate, theatre and anime.

Its shares carry securities code 9602.

Company website: https://www.toho.co.jp/

Revenue down 3.4%, SG&A up 19.8%

The table sets the first half of the fiscal year ending February 2027 beside the same period a year earlier.

Operating revenue, the line that corresponds to sales, fell by JPY 6.5bn.

Gross profit, sales minus cost of sales, fell by only JPY 1.6bn.

SG&A expenses, by contrast, rose by JPY 8.6bn.

Of the JPY 10.2bn fall in operating profit, the increase in SG&A accounts for about 84%.

This is not a simple case of films failing to sell.

2. The cost increase came in the Film and IP & Anime segments

The segment figures show what sat inside the profit decline.

Operating profit in the Film segment fell from JPY 23.2bn to JPY 19.4bn.

According to the earnings presentation, the prior-year period had big hits in "Demon Slayer" and "Kokuho", and revenue fell on the rebound.

Higher personnel and depreciation costs are also cited as reasons for the decline.

In the IP & Anime segment, revenue rose from JPY 37.313bn to JPY 43.132bn.

Even so, operating profit almost halved, from JPY 10.7bn to JPY 6.0bn.

The presentation cites one-off costs related to contingent consideration and front-loaded investment in overseas operations.

IP here means intellectual property such as anime and characters.

The company is paying, up front as expenses, to strengthen its own rollout network overseas.

So the decline reads as last year's exceptional demand reversing while advance spending for the future lands at the same time.

3. What leaving the full-year forecast alone means

The company left its full-year operating profit forecast of JPY 62.0bn unchanged.

Prior-year operating profit was JPY 67.8bn, so the forecast is for an 8.7% decline.

Now subtract the first-half figure of JPY 30.9bn.

Full-year guidance minus the first half leaves about JPY 31.1bn for the second half (September to February).

A year earlier, that figure was about JPY 26.7bn: the JPY 67.8bn full-year result minus the JPY 41.1bn first half.

The current forecast therefore assumes the second half earns about JPY 4.4bn (about 16%) more than the prior-year second half.

First-half progress against the full-year forecast is 49.9%.

A year earlier, the first half had produced about 61% of the full year, so profit now leans more towards the second half.

The company's presentation describes the first half as broadly in line with plan.

The stated reason for holding the forecast is that it wants to see how the major second-half releases open before committing.

The film "Godzilla -0.0" is scheduled for a simultaneous worldwide release in November.

All of this is the company's own explanation. Whether the second half actually beats last year will not be known until the numbers arrive.

4. The raised net income is a separate matter from the core outlook

On the same day, TOHO also revised its full-year forecasts.

Only net income attributable to parent shareholders moved.

It was raised by JPY 7.0bn (17.1%), from JPY 41.0bn to JPY 48.0bn.

Operating revenue, operating profit and ordinary profit stay at the previous forecasts.

The reason given in the timely disclosure is that part of its investment securities was sold in the third quarter (September to November).

The presentation says part of its policy-shareholdings was sold on September 15.

Policy-shareholdings are shares a company holds in other companies, typically to maintain business relationships.

The gain on the sale flows into net income as extraordinary income.

The prior-year first half also contained JPY 8.9bn of extraordinary income.

According to the presentation, that too came from selling policy-shareholdings.

This year's first half has no such extraordinary income.

The 40.2% fall in net income therefore reflects the lower operating profit plus the absence of last year's sale gain.

Full-year net income guidance of JPY 48.0bn is a 7.3% decline against the prior year's JPY 51.7bn.

The upward revision to net income is not a story about the business earning more.

Operating profit guidance, which shows the core outlook, has not changed.

Why it matters for global investors

TOHO's fiscal year ends in February, so its first half runs from March to August rather than from January to June. Ordinary profit, a line in the table above, is a Japanese-GAAP stage between operating profit and net income and does not exist under IFRS or US GAAP. Policy-shareholdings are a Japan-specific practice, and gains on selling them land in extraordinary income, which is why net income can jump in a period when operating profit falls. The filings behind these figures are published in Japanese, so the consolidated lines are worth reconciling against the primary documents.

Closing

The word "lower profit" in an earnings headline can mislead if it is read at face value.

At TOHO, costs paid up front pushed profit down by more than the fall in revenue did.

And the higher net income came from a one-off share sale.

When you look at TOHO's results, try reading operating profit and net income separately.

At the next set of results, the thing to check is whether second-half operating profit reaches the level the company assumes.
