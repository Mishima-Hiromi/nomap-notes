CaSy (9215 JP): net income up 8.3x after its first consolidated year

A Tokyo home-services matching platform, read straight from its EDINET filings

Have you ever used an app to book someone to clean your home or cook for you?

CaSy (securities code 9215) is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Growth Market in exactly that business. In its latest fiscal year, ended November 2025, net income jumped about 8.3x.

Here is what the company does, and what changed, using the numbers in its own filings.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

What the company does

CaSy Inc. (securities code 9215, Tokyo Stock Exchange Growth Market) runs "CaSy," an online matching platform for home-support services. It connects people who want cleaning or cooking help with registered staff, called "cast," through an app and a website.

Company website: https://casy.co.jp/

Business model: where the money comes from

Revenue is the usage fee users pay each time they use the service. This is not a product sold once and done; income continues as long as usage continues.

The company's website lists the fee at JPY 2,790 per hour (tax included) and up.

Of FY2025/11 revenue of JPY 1.92bn, cleaning and cooking came to JPY 1.80bn, more than nine-tenths of the total. The remaining JPY 0.12bn was other. The business is a single segment, "home-support services."

Profit is still thin: operating profit of JPY 50.98m against an operating margin of 2.7%. The path to profit was gradual. In FY2020/11 the operating loss was JPY 0.12bn, net income turned positive in FY2021/11, and operating profit has stayed positive since FY2022/11. The workforce grew from 26 people in FY2020/11 to 74.

The company itself explains on its website that it is "not a simple matching-only platform," and service contracts are signed between the user and CaSy.

The value it provides

The mission is "to create the time to treasure what matters." Order cleaning or cooking, and that time comes back to the person who asked.

Two hard-to-replace strengths can be read from the disclosed facts.

The first is geographic coverage. CaSy made Sukkiri Meister a subsidiary in February 2025 and Sanju a subsidiary in August 2025, expanding to 10 prefectures including Okinawa, Hiroshima, and Okayama. It is widening its area through acquisitions.

The second is business with government. Since fiscal 2024 it has taken part in the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's housework and childcare support program, and in October 2025 it won programs for Shibuya and Bunkyo wards. In September 2025 it was selected for the SME Growth Acceleration Subsidy.

Basic data

Here are CaSy's (9215) figures for FY2025/11. The same items appear each time.

  • Revenue: JPY 1.92bn
  • Operating profit: JPY 50.98m
  • Operating margin: 2.7%
  • Net income: JPY 46.93m
  • ROE: 17.1%
  • Equity ratio: 39.0%
  • Dividend per share: JPY 0 (no dividend)

Source: EDINET DB, annual securities report, FY2025/11.

Notable facts

The first is the jump in profit. Net income rose from JPY 5.68m in FY2024/11 to JPY 46.93m in FY2025/11, about 8.3x. Operating profit also rose from JPY 7.79m to JPY 50.98m, about 6.5x. Note, however, that the prior year was non-consolidated and the current year is the first consolidated one, so the basis of comparison is not the same. These figures should be read as including the effect of the acquisitions.

The second is a change in shareholders. Among the major shareholders in FY2025/11, the Singapore investment fund BEENEXT1 PTE. LTD. entered at ninth place for the year with 1.85%, a new holder. At the interim point it stood at tenth place with 1.90%. It was not among the top ten in FY2024/11. The largest shareholder is Watakyu Seimoa with 19.22%, a company that leases bedding and medical garments and provides laundry services mainly for hospitals and care facilities.

The third is the speed of geographic expansion. Two acquisitions took the service area to 10 prefectures.

Points to watch as an investor

The condition for further growth is that area expansion and partnerships with local governments continue. With an operating margin of 2.7% still thin, the focus is whether revenue growth can carry the margin higher.

The condition for a reversal is the burden that comes with acquisitions. Goodwill of JPY 80.56m is on the books, and weaker results at the subsidiaries could trigger an impairment. Retained earnings carry an accumulated deficit of JPY 0.31bn, and there is no dividend. The equity ratio is 39.0%.

ROE looks high at 17.1%. But shareholders' equity is small at JPY 0.27bn, so the ratio rises even when the absolute amount of profit is small.

Three things to confirm in the next earnings report. The full-year forecast for FY2026/11 is an operating loss of JPY 50.00m and a net loss of JPY 51.00m (the third-quarter earnings release filed on October 15, 2026). Third-quarter cumulative revenue was JPY 1.60bn. The questions are how the full-year loss forecast lands, what the two subsidiaries contribute, and what effect the December 1, 2026 price revision announced on the company's website has.

Why it matters for global investors

CaSy is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Growth Market, and its annual securities reports are filed on EDINET, where the underlying documents are mostly in Japanese, so a headline figure like this is easy to misread from outside Japan. The key caution here is that the 8.3x compares a non-consolidated year with the first consolidated year, so the scope changed along with the profit. The shareholder list also shows a changing base: the largest holder is Watakyu Seimoa with 19.22%, while a Singapore fund newly entered the top ten with 1.85%. When a company is this small, both the consolidation scope and the ownership mix can move the numbers as much as the business itself.
