Suzuki Motor (7269 JP): the full-year plan implies a 28% fall in second-half operating profit

First-half operating profit rose 5.5%, but the full-year forecast did not move — the difference sits in October to March

Suzuki Motor reported its April to September results on November 10, 2026.

Operating profit rose from a year earlier, and revenue was a record.

The full-year operating profit forecast, though, was left at a level 13.3% below the prior year.

If both the strong first half and the lower full-year figure are true, the difference has to sit in the second half.

This post works out what the second half has to look like.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures — earnings releases, results presentations and annual securities reports on EDINET, Japan's filing system (the counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Key points

  • Suzuki's full-year operating profit forecast for the year to March 2027 is JPY 540.0bn, 13.3% below the prior year's JPY 622.9bn. It is unchanged from the forecast given on August 5.
  • First-half operating profit was JPY 291.6bn, up 5.5% year on year. Subtract that from the full-year plan and the second half is JPY 248.4bn, 28.3% below the prior year's second half of JPY 346.4bn.
  • The plan assumes four-wheel unit sales rise 4.4% in the second half, yet operating profit falls. The operating margin goes from 8.5% in the first half to 7.1% in the second.
  • The company explains the year as covering higher raw material prices with more unit sales, mainly in India. A second-half breakdown was not in the materials reviewed.

1. What rose in the first half, and what the company changed

Suzuki Motor Corporation (https://www.globalsuzuki.com/) makes minivehicles and compact cars, motorcycles, and outboard motors.

Its Indian subsidiary, Maruti Suzuki India, accounts for a large part of four-wheel unit sales.

Operating profit is the profit earned by the core business.

The full-year forecast is the company's view of the year now running, April 2026 to March 2027.

April to September is the first half, and October to March the second.

According to the earnings release, revenue in the first half was JPY 3,419.2bn, up 19.4% from a year earlier.

Operating profit was JPY 291.6bn, up 5.5%.

Four-wheel unit sales were 1.824m, up 19.9%. India, at 1.10m units, rose 35.4% and drove the increase.

Profit attributable to owners of parent was JPY 253.7bn, up 31.6%.

That bottom line includes non-operating items such as valuation gains on financial assets. The company's supplementary materials also attribute the rise in pretax profit and interim profit to "valuation gains on financial assets, etc."

The full-year forecast issued on August 5 was changed in part only.

Revenue (JPY 6,900bn), operating profit (JPY 540.0bn) and net income (JPY 420.0bn) were left as they were.

The line that was cut is pretax profit, from JPY 720.0bn to JPY 690.0bn.

The release says this reflects valuation losses on financial assets.

In other words, the operating profit plan did not move after the first-half result. What moved was below the operating line.

So how should the unchanged JPY 540.0bn be read next to the JPY 291.6bn first half?

2. Subtract the first half from the full-year plan

Take the full-year operating profit forecast of JPY 540.0bn and subtract the first-half result of JPY 291.6bn.

The second half is JPY 248.4bn.

Run the same calculation on the prior year.

Prior-year operating profit was JPY 622.9bn for the full year and JPY 276.5bn for the first half, so the second half was JPY 346.4bn.

On that basis, this year's second half is 28.3% lower than the prior year's.

In the prior year the second half was 25% larger than the first half. This year's plan puts the second half 15% smaller than the first.

The plan assumes the relationship between the two halves flips.

This second-half figure is produced by subtraction from the full-year plan. The company did not publish a second-half number itself.

Apply the same subtraction to revenue.

The second half comes to JPY 3,480.8bn, 1.5% above the prior year's second half of JPY 3,428.7bn.

Revenue rises slightly while operating profit falls 28%, so the margin drops.

The operating margin was 9.7% in the prior-year first half and 10.1% in the prior-year second half.

This year it works out to 8.5% in the first half and 7.1% in the second.

3. The July to September quarter was already below the prior year

Set the second-half assumption against what happened inside the first half.

The company's supplementary materials include a chart of quarterly operating profit.

Read off that chart, April to June was JPY 158.0bn (prior year JPY 142.1bn) and July to September was JPY 133.6bn (prior year JPY 134.3bn).

The materials put July to September operating profit at 1% below the prior year.

Of the JPY 15.1bn first-half increase, April to June accounts for JPY 15.9bn and July to September for a decrease of JPY 0.7bn. (The quarterly figures are rounded, so they add up JPY 0.1bn off.)

Almost all of the first-half increase was concentrated in April to June.

Divide the JPY 248.4bn second-half plan by two and one quarter comes to JPY 124.2bn.

That is JPY 9.4bn below July to September's JPY 133.6bn.

If July to September's JPY 133.6bn repeated in both second-half quarters, the second half would be JPY 267.2bn and the full year JPY 558.8bn.

That is simple multiplication, not a forecast.

Still, the JPY 540.0bn plan sits JPY 18.8bn (7%) below that level.

The July to September quarter already running below the prior year, and the plan sitting further below again, point in the same direction.

The prior-year second half also included a large January to March at JPY 193.8bn, which raises the base for comparison.

Part of why this year's second-half decline looks as wide as 28% is that the prior-year second half was bigger than its first half.

4. Sales rise, so why is profit assumed to fall?

The second-half volume assumption comes from the same subtraction.

The full-year four-wheel unit forecast is 3.702m. Subtract the first-half result of 1.824m and the second half is 1.878m.

The prior-year second half was 1.798m, so that is a 4.4% increase.

Revenue rises and units rise, yet operating profit falls.

The company describes the full-year forecast this way in its supplementary materials:

Cover higher raw material prices, among other factors, with increased unit sales mainly in India, and leave operating profit unchanged.

--- Suzuki, FY2027/3 second quarter results briefing materials

The first-half drivers of operating profit are also in the materials.

Raw material price movements worked as an external factor cutting profit by JPY 98.9bn.

"Improvement in earning power" — cost reduction and changes in sales mix — added JPY 164.3bn, while growth investment and other items cut JPY 50.2bn, leaving a net increase of JPY 15.1bn. (The figures are rounded, so they add up JPY 0.1bn off.)

The release likewise says the first-half increase came "despite the negative impact of raw material price changes, on the contribution of improved earning power."

The company also writes that the impact of higher raw material prices continues to expand.

That is as far as the company's explanation goes in the materials reviewed.

How the JPY 248.4bn second-half figure is split across raw materials, foreign exchange (the full-year assumption is JPY 159 to the dollar, and the first-half actual was also JPY 159), fixed costs and the rest was not in the materials reviewed.

So the materials at hand do not allow the second-half decline to be pinned to a single cause.

Nor do they allow a judgement on whether the plan is cautious or reasonable.

Net income shows the same shape in the second half.

Subtract the first-half JPY 253.7bn from the JPY 420.0bn full-year forecast and the second half is JPY 166.3bn.

That is 32.5% below the prior-year second half of JPY 246.5bn.

This line also includes the non-operating valuation losses.

Why it matters for global investors

Japanese listed companies mostly close their financial year on March 31, so the year labelled FY2027/3 in this post runs from April 2026 to March 2027, and results and guidance are split into a first half (April to September) and a second half (October to March). Many of them guide only on the full year, which means the second-half figure usually has to be derived by subtraction, as here — and that derived figure is an inference, not a company disclosure. The underlying documents, the earnings release and the briefing materials, are filed in Japanese; EDINET, the Financial Services Agency's disclosure system, is where the filings themselves are published. Suzuki's four-wheel volumes are weighted toward India through Maruti Suzuki India, so the India sales figures in these materials are the same volumes that sit behind the consolidated numbers reported in yen.

Closing

Read only the headline of a higher first-half profit and the year looks on track.

Subtract the first half from the full-year plan, though, and the numbers show how the company sees the second half.

Suzuki left its full-year operating profit forecast unchanged and placed the second half below the first.

Next time results come out, check how far third-quarter operating profit, for October to December, clears the JPY 124.2bn average.
