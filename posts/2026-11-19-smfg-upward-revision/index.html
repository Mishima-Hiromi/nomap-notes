SMFG (8316 JP): net income forecast raised to JPY 1.9tn, half of it environmental

The company's own breakdown puts JPY 100bn of the JPY 200bn upgrade down to rate rises and one-off items

Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group raised its consolidated net income forecast for the fiscal year ending March 2027 to JPY 1.9tn.

In the six months from April to September 2026 it earned more than JPY 1tn, and it lifted the full-year outlook by JPY 200bn from the plan set at the start of the year.

But the company itself has published what the increase is made of.

Half of the JPY 200bn comes from the environment and one-off factors such as higher interest rates, it says.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • SMFG raised its net income forecast for the fiscal year ending March 2027 from JPY 1.7tn to JPY 1.9tn.
  • In the company's own breakdown, half of the +JPY 200bn comes from the environment and one-off factors such as higher rates, and the other half from what it calls real growth.
  • The first-half increase was driven mainly by higher net interest income, so reading the whole gain as real strength is premature.

1. A first half above JPY 1tn for the first time

Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group (securities code 8316) is a financial group built around Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation, with securities, trust banking, cards and leasing businesses.

Company website: https://www.smfg.co.jp

In its second-quarter (interim) results for the fiscal year ending March 2027, announced on November 13, 2026, net income attributable to owners of parent was JPY 1,003.2bn.

That was up JPY 69.7bn from JPY 933.5bn a year earlier, and the company says it is the first time its half-year profit has exceeded JPY 1tn.

The full-year net income forecast is JPY 1.9tn.

That is a JPY 200bn upgrade from the initial JPY 1.7tn, and 20.0% higher than the prior year.

The market consensus compiled by IBES and reported by Reuters was JPY 1.84tn.

The new forecast is above it, but the gap is only about JPY 60bn.

The dividend also rose.

The annual dividend forecast is JPY 100 per share on a post-stock-split basis, up from JPY 90.

The company also decided on a buyback of up to JPY 220bn.

2. The company's own split between environment and real strength

The company published the breakdown of the upgrade in its investor presentation on November 19, 2026.

Of the JPY 200bn, JPY 100bn is what it calls environment and one-off factors.

Higher interest rates account for +JPY 95bn.

Gains on sales of strategic shareholdings (stakes held to maintain relationships with business partners) add +JPY 35bn.

Accelerating the reduction of low-profit assets takes off JPY 30bn.

Together they come to JPY 100bn.

The remaining JPY 100bn is classified as real growth.

The JPY 95bn from rates comes from replacing an assumption: the initial plan assumed a domestic policy rate of 0.75%, and the company now uses 1.25%.

In other words, that part did not grow because the business grew. It grew because the assumption underneath the forecast changed.

At a press briefing, President Takeshi Nakajima said about half of the increase came from environmental factors such as higher rates, and that the remaining JPY 100bn reflected a strong core business.

He said he took the remaining JPY 100bn as encouraging.

3. A 0.25% rate rise adds JPY 110bn in the first year

The company puts a number on how much rates move profit.

A 0.25% rise in the policy rate adds JPY 110bn to revenue in the first year.

By the fifth year, as fixed-rate loans reprice, the lift reaches JPY 150bn.

The JPY 1.9tn forecast assumes a policy rate of 1.25%.

Nakajima said he sees a high likelihood that the Bank of Japan raises rates to around 1.75%.

Two 0.25% hikes would, on a simple doubling of the first-year estimate, add JPY 220bn.

The company, however, expects competition to gather deposits to intensify as rates rise.

So the size of the tailwind depends not only on rates but on how much in deposits the group can collect.

4. What drove the first-half profit

Breaking down the first-half profit and loss makes the increase easier to see.

Consolidated net interest income was JPY 1,620bn, up JPY 337.1bn from a year earlier.

Fee and commission income was JPY 938.4bn, up JPY 109.1bn.

Operating expenses were JPY 1,412.8bn, up JPY 184.0bn.

Gains and losses on stocks and other securities were JPY 161.6bn, down JPY 84.7bn, mainly because the prior year included a JPY 94bn pre-tax gain on the sale of shares in India's Kotak Mahindra Bank.

Credit-related costs were JPY 133.5bn, up JPY 43.4bn.

The final increase was JPY 69.7bn.

The source of the profit growth was the JPY 337.1bn increase in net interest income.

The company attributes the increase to higher domestic loan balances and the tailwind from higher rates.

At Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation on a standalone basis, the loan yield rose to 1.68%, and the loan-deposit spread widened by 0.24 points from a year earlier to 1.34%.

The growth in balances and the rise in rates are not separated in the earnings figures.

5. Why real strength is not zero

That said, the remaining JPY 100bn of real strength is not fictional.

Olive is the digital retail financial service that Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation started ahead of other banks.

The JPY 109.1bn increase in fee and commission income was helped by capturing active corporate actions such as M&A, and by higher transaction volume at Olive.

Olive reached 8.6mn accounts between March 2023 and September 2026, and personal deposits rose from JPY 57.9tn to JPY 63.1tn.

Trunk, the service for small and medium-sized companies and newly incorporated firms, has applications from one in four new companies.

The cost ratio was 51.5%, an improvement of 1.9 points from a year earlier.

Cost growth is being absorbed as the top line expands.

These are the elements that support the JPY 100bn the company calls real strength.

The company also cites strong markets as a driver of the profit increase.

Operating net profit by segment moved by +JPY 92.4bn in markets, +JPY 97.5bn in retail, +JPY 134.8bn in wholesale and -JPY 47.7bn in global compared with a year earlier.

In its presentation, the company lists further policy rate hikes alongside continued favorable momentum, including active corporate actions and high stock prices, as changes in the environment.

The company itself counts the strong market and deal flow as one of its current tailwinds.

While that boom lasts it looks like real strength. If it cools, it may not show up in the same form.

6. How to read JPY 1.9tn

The JPY 1.9tn figure looks different once the environmental part is stripped out.

Add only the JPY 100bn of real growth to the initial JPY 1.7tn forecast and you get JPY 1.8tn.

That is close to the IBES consensus of JPY 1.84tn.

The JPY 100bn of environment and one-off factors, however, is different in nature.

The gain on sales of strategic shareholdings (+JPY 35bn) is classified by the company as a one-off item.

The JPY 95bn from rates rests on the 1.25% policy rate assumption, and it would change if the assumption breaks.

The bottom-line net income target for FY2028 is JPY 2tn. The presentation assumes a policy rate of 1.25% and USD/JPY 150 for the JPY 1.9tn forecast (from FY2026 onward).

The current forecast is already JPY 1.9tn, leaving a gap of only JPY 100bn to the target.

Nakajima said that if the current economic environment continues, the mid-term management plan will need to be updated next May.

The presentation also says the mid-term target will be updated when full-year results are announced.

The increase in profit is real.

But half of its source is the environment, and the other half is partly supported by strong markets.

To see real strength, it is better to follow fee and commission income, deposits and the cost ratio.

Why it matters for global investors

SMFG's filings and presentation materials are published in Japanese, and the underlying documents sit on EDINET, the Financial Services Agency's filing system, which is why the numbers above are read from Japanese-language primary sources. For a Japanese megabank in a rate-rise cycle, the policy rate embedded in guidance matters as much as the headline profit: the company says a 0.25% move is worth JPY 110bn in the first year. That sensitivity, the split between environment and real growth, and gains on strategic shareholdings are the items to watch when comparing a headline forecast with the business underneath it. Japanese fiscal years end in March, so FY2027/3 runs from April 2026 to March 2027.

Closing

Earnings headlines are always dressed in big numbers.

But break down where the number came from, and the picture changes.

When the rate tailwind stops, what remains at this company?

That is what to watch in the next earnings report.
