Takeuchi (6432 JP): revenue up 16.0%, operating profit up 3.4%, and a JPY 15bn buyback

A Japanese compact-excavator maker pairs a modest profit gain with a bigger payout

2026-10-18 · 日本語版

Hearing that operating profit rose 3.4% may make the growth sound small.

Takeuchi Manufacturing, a maker of compact excavators, on the same day approved a JPY 15bn share buyback and a dividend increase.

Why return more to shareholders when profit growth is modest?

Following the results in order shows what the company was looking at when it made that call.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from Japanese-language earnings releases on TDnet to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • For the six months from March to August 2026, Takeuchi's revenue rose 16.0% while operating profit rose 3.4%.
  • One main factor holding profit growth back was US tariffs, which pushed first-half operating profit down by JPY 1.728bn.
  • On the same day, the company raised full-year operating profit guidance from JPY 37.3bn to JPY 44.5bn, lifted the annual dividend to JPY 245, and approved a buyback of up to JPY 15bn.
  • It built JPY 6.8bn of full-year tariff refunds and a weaker yen into profit, and with a thick capital base decided to return surplus cash to shareholders.

1. Revenue rose 16.0%, but operating profit rose only 3.4%

What Takeuchi makes

Takeuchi Manufacturing makes compact hydraulic excavators (mini excavators) and crawler loaders, construction machinery that scoops and carries earth and sand.

Its shares carry securities code 6432 and trade on the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

North America and Europe are its main markets, and it sells machines through local sales subsidiaries and distributors.

Company website: https://www.takeuchiglobal.com/

First-half results

The table sets the first-half results for March to August 2026 beside the same period a year earlier.

Revenue rose from JPY 114.10bn to JPY 132.38bn, up 16.0%.

Operating profit rose from JPY 23.40bn to JPY 24.20bn, up 3.4%.

Ordinary profit (recurring profit) rose 6.0%, from JPY 23.69bn to JPY 25.12bn, and net income attributable to owners of the parent rose 6.3%, from JPY 17.06bn to JPY 18.13bn.

Revenue grew 16.0% while operating profit grew only 3.4%.

Growth came in, but profit did not keep pace with it.

2. US tariffs held profit growth back

The earnings release explains, in the company's own words, why operating profit struggled.

US tariffs cut operating profit by JPY 1.728bn.

The breakdown is a JPY 7.684bn increase in tariff costs, JPY 3.331bn passed through into prices, and JPY 2.625bn of tariff refunds.

Part of the higher tariff cost was covered by price increases and refunds.

Higher ocean freight, tied to rising crude oil prices, also weighed on profit.

By region, US segment revenue rose 18.5% to JPY 75.38bn, while segment profit fell 4.3% to JPY 4.24bn.

Besides tariffs, the company cites a higher share of sales to large rental companies, which qualify for volume discounts.

More revenue does not mean the same proportion of it is left as profit.

3. The company raised full-year operating profit guidance from JPY 37.3bn to JPY 44.5bn

On the same day, the company also published a notice on revisions to its earnings and dividend forecasts.

It raised full-year operating profit guidance for the fiscal year ending February 2027 from JPY 37.3bn at the April stage to JPY 44.5bn.

Revenue guidance rose from JPY 244.0bn to JPY 261.0bn. Operating profit in the prior fiscal year was JPY 37.69bn.

The main reason for the raise is US tariff refunds.

The company expects refunds to lift operating profit by JPY 2.6bn in the second quarter, JPY 4.2bn in the third quarter and JPY 6.8bn for the full year.

Tariffs taken as a cost in the first half are expected to come back as refunds in the second.

A weaker yen also helps. The second-half assumption is JPY 150 per US dollar, weaker than JPY 147 in the previous forecast.

On the other hand, fuel surcharges, higher ocean freight from Panama Canal transit restrictions, and discounts to promote sales in the United States are built in as factors that push profit down.

Orders received rose 48.1% year on year to JPY 145.98bn, and the order backlog rose to JPY 57.17bn.

The company says, however, that it will stop disclosing orders received and order backlog with the fiscal year ending February 2027, because quarterly swings have become large and their usefulness as reference information has diminished.

4. Even with slower profit growth, it went ahead with shareholder returns

On the same day, the company also increased returns to shareholders.

First, the dividend. It raised the annual dividend forecast from JPY 220 to JPY 245. The prior-year actual was JPY 210.

The year-end dividend rises from JPY 110 to JPY 135. Until now the company paid a single year-end dividend; an interim dividend is added, making two payments a year from this fiscal year.

Second, the buyback. The board approved the acquisition of up to 2.2m shares, equal to 4.8% of shares outstanding excluding treasury shares, for a total acquisition cost of up to JPY 15bn.

Purchases run from October 14, 2026 to February 19, 2027, through market purchases on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

Under the current shareholder return policy, taking into account the share price level and capital efficiency, in order to provide shareholders with a further return of profits

--- Takeuchi Manufacturing, "Notice regarding decision on matters concerning acquisition of treasury shares" (October 13, 2026)

The company's basic policy is to give cash flow priority for growth investment, secure working capital, and then apply surplus cash to shareholder returns.

On that basis, it aims to raise its consolidated payout ratio to 40% in stages.

In a first half with modest profit growth, it decided on both a dividend increase and a buyback.

5. What supports the payout is a thick capital base

Where does the money for the returns come from?

First-half operating cash flow was an outflow of JPY 11.11bn. In the same period a year earlier it was an inflow of JPY 3.55bn.

The main factor was a JPY 24.05bn increase in receivables. Sales grew, and money not yet collected piled up.

Cash and cash equivalents were JPY 34.24bn, down JPY 21.99bn from the end of the previous fiscal year.

Even so, the equity ratio was 83.9% and net assets were JPY 198.72bn.

The company's basic policy was to secure working capital and put surplus cash into returns.

Higher receivables and inventories count as an increase in working capital, and until that money is collected, capital and cash on hand support it.

A 3.4% rise in first-half operating profit was not a reason to stop returning cash.

Read the other way, the company could act because its capital was thick and a second-half return from tariff refunds was in sight.

Why it matters for global investors

Takeuchi's shares trade on the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange, and the company reports on a fiscal year ending in February, so the "first half" here runs from March to August rather than January to June. The half-year earnings release, the forecast revision and the buyback notice were published in Japanese, while the company also maintains an investor relations site in English. Japanese companies file their annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's disclosure system, and many of those documents are published only in Japanese.

Closing

What the company was looking at was not only the first-half operating profit growth.

It was the tariff refunds coming back in the second half, and the thick capital base.

That is why it could decide to return cash to shareholders through a dividend increase and a buyback even with modest profit growth.

Looking past the headline numbers in the results, at what the company built in and what it decided, makes the reasoning visible.
