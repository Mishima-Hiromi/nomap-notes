Sumitomo Metal Mining (5713 JP): from a 94% profit drop to a 9.69x rebound

Three years of falling profit, then a sharp reversal, read straight from EDINET filings

2026-09-26 · 日本語版

If profit had fallen for three years in a row, would you assume the company was done for a while?

Sumitomo Metal Mining's latest results record a sharp turn away from exactly that.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

What the company does

Sumitomo Metal Mining (securities code 5713, Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime Market) is a resource and materials maker in the Sumitomo group. It handles nonferrous metals such as copper and nickel end to end, from mine development through smelting to battery materials.

Company website: https://www.smm.co.jp

1. Net income fell about 94%, then rebounded 9.69x

Net income shows how wide the swings are.

Net income in FY2022 was JPY 281.04bn, the highest on record at the time.

Three years later, in FY2025, it had fallen to JPY 16.49bn. That is a decline of about 94%.

In FY2026 net income came back to JPY 176.29bn.

That is a 9.69x rebound from the previous year.

2. A dividend larger than the year's profit

The bottom year, FY2025, holds one more surprising number.

Against net income of JPY 16.49bn, total dividends were JPY 28.59bn.

The payout ratio was 173.36%. In other words, the company paid out more in dividends than it earned that year.

The decision kept to the target in its medium-term plan of a dividend on equity (DOE) ratio of at least 2.5%.

3. The medium-term plan target was reached a year early

Medium-Term Plan 2027 set income before tax of JPY 140.0bn as the target for fiscal 2027.

One year before the target year, FY2026 actual income before tax was JPY 255.68bn.

That is about 1.8x the target, so the goal was met a year ahead of schedule.

Why it matters for global investors

Everything above comes from the company's annual securities reports, which are filed on EDINET in Japanese. For a company whose profit swings this widely, the filings show something a single year's results cannot: here, the dividend was held at a level above that year's profit, in line with the company's stated DOE target. Reading several years of filings side by side is how that policy becomes visible.

Closing

Holding the dividend at the bottom, then passing the plan's target a year early: the size of the swings describes this company well.
