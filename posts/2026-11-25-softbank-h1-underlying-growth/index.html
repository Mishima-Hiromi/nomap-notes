SoftBank Corp. (9434 JP): operating profit up 2%, or 13% once last year's one-off is excluded

A JPY 58.9bn remeasurement gain in the prior-year first half holds the headline number down

An earnings headline saying operating profit rose 2% year on year makes the growth look small.

For SoftBank Corp.'s first half, that headline number does not tell you what happened.

The prior year contained a large one-time gain.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from earnings materials to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • SoftBank Corp.'s first half of the fiscal year ending March 2027 posted operating profit of JPY 642.1bn, up 2% from a year earlier.
  • Excluding a JPY 58.9bn one-off gain booked in the prior-year first half, the increase is 13%.
  • The headline 2% is low only because the prior-year base looks large; the growth against that base is stronger than the headline.
  • The current period, however, may also contain a gain on a sale, and the amount cannot be confirmed from the materials.

1. What was inside the prior-year comparison

SoftBank Corp. (securities code 9434) is a major company built around mobile and other telecommunications.

Its businesses are divided into Consumer, Enterprise, Distribution, Media & EC, and Financial (official site: https://www.softbank.jp/corp/).

It is a listed company separate from its parent holding company, SoftBank Group (9984).

The first-half (April to September) figures the company presented at its earnings briefing on November 9, 2026 are as follows.

Revenue was JPY 3,798.7bn, up 12% year on year.

Operating profit was JPY 642.1bn, up 2%.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent was JPY 364.5bn, up 5%.

Revenue, operating profit and net income were all first-half records.

Operating profit is the only line with small growth, because the prior-year period it is measured against was exceptional.

In a note in its materials, the company explains that the prior-year first half included "a gain of JPY 58.9bn from remeasurement accompanying the business combination of LINE MAN CORPORATION PTE. LTD. and LINE Bank Taiwan Limited."

Excluding the one-off impact in the prior-year period, up 13%

--- SoftBank Corp., "FY2027/3 second-quarter earnings briefing summary"

The same operating profit can be read as either up 2% or up 13%.

2. Is a remeasurement gain the same as profit from the business?

A remeasurement gain is an accounting gain that arises when a stake a company already holds is revalued at fair value as it turns that company into a subsidiary.

It is not profit earned by selling goods or services.

Nor is there any guarantee the same amount appears the following year.

That is why the company also presents a growth rate with the JPY 58.9bn taken out of the prior year.

Working back from the disclosed percentages gives prior-year operating profit of roughly JPY 630bn, or about JPY 570bn excluding the one-off.

Against JPY 570bn, this year's JPY 642.1bn is up about 13%, which is consistent with the company's explanation.

Because this is an estimate back-calculated from rounded percentages, check the exact prior-year figure in the earnings release.

On the discussion so far, the headline 2% is low by the amount of the prior year's JPY 58.9bn, and growth excluding the prior-year one-off reads as 13%.

3. Which businesses carried the growth

The segments the company's materials describe as having increased profit are Enterprise, Distribution and Financial.

Media & EC is up once the prior-year one-off is excluded.

Consumer, the largest segment by prior-year operating profit (JPY 550.8bn), was down.

Enterprise revenue rose 12% year on year and profit rose 20%.

The company says revenue in cloud and AI rose 37% and that earnings from AI computing infrastructure drove the growth.

Financial revenue rose 27% and profit rose 74%.

Media & EC profit rose 16% excluding the prior-year one-off.

Including the one-off, it is down 25%.

The materials indicate that the prior-year one-off affected profit in this segment.

The 13% overall figure does not rest on one strong segment; it comes from several segments increasing profit.

Consumer revenue rose 9% while profit fell 2%.

The company says profit progress against the full-year forecast is 58%, on track toward higher full-year profit.

The materials do not give the reason for the decline.

On the company's forecast page, which sets FY2026/3 results alongside the current forecast, Consumer carries a note that amortisation of past years' sales commissions and promotional costs will increase.

Whether the first-half decline comes from that cannot be confirmed from the materials.

4. Is there a reason to discount the 13%?

To state the conclusion first: as a comparison with the prior year, 13% is closer than the headline 2%.

But 13% is the company's own figure, and there are two reasons to discount it.

The first is a one-off in the current period.

The company raised its full-year forecast this time.

It lifted operating profit from JPY 1,100bn to JPY 1,160bn and net income from JPY 560bn to JPY 610bn.

Alongside strong business progress, the reasons include a gain on the sale of preferred interests in Energy Global, LP.

The forecast page says the gain is reflected in the interim consolidated period of FY2027/3.

Whether the gain sits in operating profit, and how much of it falls in the first half, cannot be confirmed from the materials.

If the current period contains a gain of the same kind, the 13%, which strips out only the prior-year one-off, may be somewhat flattered.

The second is that excluding the prior-year item is the company's own judgment.

The company decides which items to call one-off.

The JPY 58.9bn here is clearly a remeasurement from a business combination, so the reason for excluding it is easy to explain.

Even so, the adjusted figure is the company's chosen presentation, and it is worth reading alongside the numbers in the earnings release.

The full-year forecast of JPY 1,160bn in operating profit is 11% above the FY2026/3 actual of JPY 1,042.6bn.

The first-half 13% and the full-year forecast 11% are at similar levels.

The growth pace the company expects for the year and the first-half 13% point the same way.

The full-year forecast, however, may include the sale gain mentioned above.

The briefing also covered an outage caused by unauthorised access to some IDC Frontier systems.

495 companies and local governments subscribing to IDCF Cloud were affected, but the company says no data inside customers' virtual servers leaked out.

How the incident will affect results is not described in the materials.

Why it matters for global investors

SoftBank Corp. (9434) is a separate listed company from SoftBank Group (9984), the holding company most non-Japanese investors know by the SoftBank name; the two report separately, and a first-half result at one is not a result for the other.

Japanese interim earnings materials are usually published only in Japanese, and the adjusted growth rate a company cites, such as the 13% here, tends to sit in a note or in the briefing summary rather than in the headline of the earnings release.

When a Japanese company reports low single-digit profit growth, it is worth checking the prior-year base for one-off items before reading the number as a slowdown.

Closing

The headline figures in interim results change a great deal depending on what was in the prior year.

"Up 2%" and "up 13%" are two ways of reading the same set of results.

The useful question is not which is right but what was excluded to arrive at the number.

At SoftBank Corp., what was excluded was a one-time gain in the prior year, and the reason for excluding it is disclosed.

When a company you follow reports, look past the headline percentage and read the notes for one-off items.
