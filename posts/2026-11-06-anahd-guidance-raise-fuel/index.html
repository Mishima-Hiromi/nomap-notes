ANA Holdings (9202 JP): revenue forecast raised JPY 100bn, operating profit held at JPY 150bn

The extra revenue meets a matching rise in operating expenses, JPY 76bn of it fuel

Hear that ANA raised its full-year forecast, and it is easy to assume profit goes up with it.

Read the contents, though, and operating profit was not raised. What went up was revenue, and the profit lines below operating profit.

The operating profit forecast stays at JPY 150bn.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from earnings materials to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • ANA Holdings raised its revenue forecast for the fiscal year ending March 2027 from JPY 2,770bn to JPY 2,870bn on October 29, 2026, and left operating profit at JPY 150bn.
  • In the first half (April to September), consolidated revenue rose 17.5% while operating profit fell 9.7%, which the company attributes to a sharp increase in fuel costs.
  • The full-year operating expense forecast rises by the same JPY 100bn as revenue, and fuel cost and fuel tax in the air transport segment account for JPY 76bn of that. The extra sales do not stay in profit.
  • First-half operating profit beat the plan by a wide margin, yet the full-year forecast did not move. On the company's own numbers, the second half is much thinner than the first.

1. What the company raised, and what it left alone

ANA Holdings (securities code 9202) is the holding company of an airline group that includes ANA, Peach Aviation and Nippon Cargo Airlines (NCA).

Company website: https://www.anahd.co.jp/

On October 29 it published second-quarter results for the fiscal year ending March 2027 (April 2026 to March 2027), and with them revised its full-year consolidated forecast.

A company forecast is the full-year outlook the company itself publishes.

Revenue rose by JPY 100bn, net income by JPY 3bn and ordinary profit by JPY 2bn.

Operating profit stayed at JPY 150bn.

The operating margin, operating profit as a share of revenue, fell from 5.4% to 5.2%.

The headline on the company's own presentation says it will absorb higher fuel costs by capturing strong demand and using a flexible pricing strategy, and hold operating profit at JPY 150bn.

So the company's own framing is that it defended profit, not that it added to it.

The materials give three reasons for the revision: strong international passenger demand reflected in the plan, changed foreign exchange and fuel assumptions for the second half, and first-half results including the sale of strategic shareholdings.

With operating profit unchanged, the JPY 3bn increase in net income comes from lines below operating profit.

The breakdown was not shown as a total figure in the materials available.

2. First half: revenue up 17.5%, operating profit down

The first-half results show what stands behind the unchanged forecast.

Consolidated revenue went from JPY 1,190.4bn to JPY 1,398.7bn, an increase of JPY 208.3bn (17.5%), and was a record for a first half.

Operating profit fell from JPY 97.6bn to JPY 88.1bn, down JPY 9.4bn (9.7%).

Net income attributable to owners of the parent also fell, from JPY 76bn to JPY 67bn, a decline of 11.8%.

Behind the lower profit is a rise in fuel costs.

Fuel cost and fuel tax in the air transport segment went from JPY 223.3bn to JPY 360.1bn, an increase of JPY 136.7bn.

The company explains that fuel costs rose sharply from a year earlier because of the situation in the Middle East.

The materials are equally clear on why revenue grew.

In international passenger services, inbound and outbound demand was strong. In international cargo, AI-related demand was strong and cargo unit prices rose.

ANA's international passenger revenue was JPY 492bn, up 21% from a year earlier, and international cargo revenue (ANA plus Nippon Cargo Airlines) was JPY 217.5bn, up 78%.

Nippon Cargo Airlines was consolidated from the second quarter of the previous year, so the cargo increase also includes the wider scope of consolidation.

Revenue grew sharply, but the fuel increase of JPY 136.7bn cancelled out a large part of it.

That is the main content of the first-half decline in profit.

There is one point worth not missing here.

First-half operating profit was a large beat against the company's initial plan.

Air transport segment operating profit was JPY 80.1bn against a first-half plan of JPY 31.5bn, JPY 48.5bn above plan.

Lower than the prior year and sharply above the plan: two views of the same half sit side by side.

3. The extra JPY 100bn of revenue disappears into the same amount of cost

Look at the full-year forecast, and the increase in revenue and the increase in operating expenses come out at exactly the same amount.

The revenue forecast rose by JPY 100bn, and the operating expense forecast also rose by JPY 100bn, from JPY 2,620bn to JPY 2,720bn.

Of the increase in operating expenses, JPY 76bn is fuel cost and fuel tax in the air transport segment.

That forecast went from JPY 642bn to JPY 718bn.

The prior-year actual was JPY 472.3bn, so against the prior year the increase is JPY 245.6bn.

The fuel price assumption the company now uses for the second half is also higher.

For Singapore kerosene, the international benchmark for jet fuel, it changed the second-half assumption from USD 90 to USD 150 per barrel.

The first-half actual was USD 160.

The initial plan assumed USD 200 in the first quarter and USD 120 in the second.

Split the fuel increase between the halves, and the difference from the initial plan is minus JPY 13.5bn in the first half and plus JPY 89.5bn in the second.

In the first half the market ran below the assumption, so fuel cost came in below plan.

In the second half the assumption has been raised, and that weight lands there.

On the revenue side, the increase is centred on international services.

The air transport segment revenue forecast rose by JPY 90bn. The increases are JPY 46bn for ANA international passenger, JPY 28bn for cargo and mail, JPY 22.5bn for Nippon Cargo Airlines and JPY 5.5bn for Peach.

ANA domestic passenger fell by JPY 7bn and other fell by JPY 5bn, and the net is JPY 90bn.

The unchanged operating profit is not one solid block either.

The air transport segment operating profit forecast went from JPY 150bn to JPY 145bn, down JPY 5bn.

Meanwhile air transport-related went from JPY 8bn to JPY 12bn and travel from JPY 0 to JPY 1bn, together covering JPY 5bn.

Consolidated operating profit did not move because increases elsewhere filled the JPY 5bn cut in air transport.

When fuel rises, international passenger services are outside the hedge book, and the company's principle is to respond through the fuel surcharge, the levy added to fares, the company explains.

With the current sharp spike in the market, the impact does not settle at a constant ratio, so the company has withheld disclosure of the sensitivity of profit to fuel price changes.

As a result, if the second-half fuel price moves away from the USD 150 assumption, the effect on profit cannot be calculated from the materials.

4. The second-half forecast cancels out the first-half beat

The first half came in JPY 48.5bn above plan.

Even so, the full-year air transport operating profit forecast was cut by only JPY 5bn.

For both to hold at once, second-half profit has to be seen as much lower than first assumed.

Take the first-half actual of JPY 80.1bn in air transport operating profit away from the revised full-year forecast of JPY 145bn, and the second half is JPY 64.9bn.

Take the first-half plan of JPY 31.5bn away from the initial full-year forecast of JPY 150bn, and the initial second half was JPY 118.5bn.

The difference is about JPY 53.5bn, which is close to the first-half beat of JPY 48.5bn plus the JPY 5bn cut to the full year.

Profit built in the first half does not remain in the second-half forecast. Fuel cost is expected to be JPY 89.5bn higher than the initial plan in the second half.

At the consolidated level, operating profit is JPY 88.1bn in the first half and JPY 61.9bn in the second, which is JPY 150bn minus JPY 88.1bn.

A year earlier, the second half was JPY 119.8bn, the full-year JPY 217.4bn minus the first-half JPY 97.6bn.

This year's second half is about half of last year's.

The company also says that for international passenger services it has made its view of price elasticity, how far demand falls as fuel surcharges rise, smaller than in the first half, and built that into the forecast.

What can be written here is only the second-half picture the company's forecast shows.

Whether fuel prices and demand land on the assumptions cannot be judged from these materials.

The end of the materials also says results could differ materially from the forecast because of moves in exchange rates, demand and prices.

Read the "upward revision" headline alone as profit heading higher, and you end up away from the numbers the company actually showed.

Why it matters for global investors

Japanese companies close their books on March 31, so FY2027/3 runs from April 2026 to March 2027 and the first half is April to September. ANA Holdings publishes its quarterly presentation and most of its disclosure in Japanese only, and operating expenses is the company's own aggregation that contains fuel cost and fuel tax, which is why the offset can only be checked by matching that line. In Japan, any mid-year raise to a company's own full-year outlook is reported as an "upward revision" whichever line moves, so it is worth checking first whether revenue or operating profit was the line that changed. Statutory filings are published on EDINET, Japan's FSA filing system (the counterpart to SEC EDGAR).

Closing

An upward revision to revenue and the movement in profit have to be read separately.

The linked Japanese version sets this against two other cases: one where the operating profit forecast was left alone, and one where it was raised.

The ANA Holdings announcement is an example where the "upward revision" headline and the actual figure, operating profit still at JPY 150bn, did not line up.

Next time an airline raises its forecast, check first whether revenue or operating profit went up.

Then look at where operating expenses increased, and the gap between the headline and the numbers becomes visible.
