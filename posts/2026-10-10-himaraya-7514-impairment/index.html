Himaraya (7514 JP): a net loss of JPY 0.86bn while operating profit edged up

A JPY 0.98bn impairment pushed the sports retailer into the red, but the core business held about flat

A headline saying "revised to a net loss" makes it look as if the core business had broken down.

Himaraya's disclosure of October 9, 2026 shows a slightly different picture.

Here are the numbers, one at a time.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from earnings releases to annual securities reports on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • For the fiscal year ending August 2026, Himaraya moved from a forecast net profit of JPY 0.32bn to a forecast net loss of JPY 0.86bn.
  • Operating profit, however, is forecast at JPY 0.29bn, above the prior year's JPY 0.285bn. The main cause of the loss is a JPY 0.98bn impairment loss.
  • Ordinary profit, meanwhile, has shrunk from JPY 2.22bn to JPY 0.32bn over five years, and the mid-term plan's target was missed. The company is also dropping numerical targets for the time being.
  • The point is not the size of the loss. It is that the profit level has stayed low.

What Himaraya is

Himaraya (securities code 7514) is a sporting goods retailer that started in Gifu Prefecture.

It was founded in 1976 selling ski equipment and general sporting goods. Its shares are listed on the Standard Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange and the Premier Market of the Nagoya Stock Exchange.

Company website: https://www.hmry.jp/

1. What moved between the two forecasts

The consolidated forecast for the fiscal year ending August 2026 was revised from the figures published on October 10, 2025.

Revenue was cut by JPY 0.9bn, from JPY 62.0bn to JPY 61.1bn.

Operating profit went from JPY 0.44bn to JPY 0.29bn.

Net income turned from a JPY 0.32bn profit into a JPY 0.86bn loss, a swing of JPY 1.18bn.

Operating profit fell by JPY 0.15bn. The swing in net income is JPY 1.18bn, close to 8x that.

Where did the difference come from?

2. The gap is a JPY 0.98bn impairment loss

According to the company's disclosure, it will book a new JPY 0.98bn as an impairment loss under extraordinary losses.

Together with the amount already recorded through the third quarter, impairment losses for the fiscal year ending August 2026 come to JPY 1.16bn.

An impairment loss is an accounting entry. When the amount an asset such as a store is expected to earn in future falls below its book value, the difference is written off as a loss. No cash leaves the company.

Operating profit is forecast at JPY 0.29bn, against the prior year's JPY 0.285bn.

The disclosure says operating profit has recovered to the point of exceeding the prior year's result.

So this year's loss is not a loss in the core business. An accounting loss, the impairment, pushed net income down.

3. The impairment is about where e-commerce profit is booked

The reason for the impairment is a little unusual.

According to the disclosure, profitability declined at some stores.

In addition, part of the e-commerce products that had been shipped from stores will be consolidated into warehouse shipping.

Through that consolidation, part of the e-commerce sales profit that had been booked at stores is expected to move to the logistics and warehouse division.

As a result, the company decided to impair the stores whose profitability had fallen.

Sales that used to count as a store's result will now count as the warehouse's result. That lowers the estimated earning power of each individual store.

What this suggests is not that the company's overall earnings suddenly fell, but that the value placed on stores fell because the earnings are now booked somewhere else.

The disclosure does not give impairment amounts per store, or store names.

4. The uncomfortable part is the level of profit itself

Here is the ordinary profit trend.

On an EDINET annual securities report basis, ordinary profit was JPY 2.22bn in FY2021 and JPY 2.37bn in FY2022.

It then fell to JPY 1.13bn in FY2023, JPY 0.43bn in FY2024 and JPY 0.38bn in FY2025.

This year's forecast is JPY 0.32bn, about one-seventh of the FY2021 level.

Even if operating profit does exceed the prior year, the margin is JPY 0.005bn (JPY 5 million).

In ordinary profit terms, the forecast falls from JPY 0.382bn to JPY 0.324bn, a decline of 15.2%.

The impairment trend, side by side:

Through FY2025 the figure ran between JPY 0.04bn and JPY 0.32bn a year. This year it is expected to be JPY 1.16bn, more than 3x the prior year.

The figures through FY2025 come from annual securities reports on EDINET. FY2026 is the company's own estimate.

The re-examination of store profitability may not be a one-year story.

5. The mid-term plan was missed, and numerical targets are being set aside

The end of the disclosure covers the outcome of the mid-term plan.

The final-year target, as revised on October 10, 2025, was ordinary profit of JPY 0.50bn.

The result is expected to be JPY 0.32bn. The target was missed.

The company gives as the reason a first-half slump caused by the long heat and mild winter that continued from the previous year.

Sales of racket sports and shoes, it says, were strong.

The plan going forward is also clear.

Structural inflation and uncertainty from climate change are expected to continue beyond next year.

The company will not publish numerical mid-term targets for the time being, and will concentrate on reliably achieving its single-year forecasts.

As a matter of management responsibility, the representative director's monthly remuneration will be cut by 40% for three months, and that of other directors (excluding audit and supervisory committee members) by 20% for three months.

Even if the impairment looks separate from the core business, the company itself acknowledges the missed target and shows responsibility through pay cuts.

On the numbers, this year's loss has a strong one-off accounting character.

What really deserves attention, though, is the five-year path in which operating profit stayed almost flat while ordinary profit shrank.

Why it matters for global investors

Himaraya's fiscal year ends in August, so FY2026 means the twelve months to August 2026, not the calendar year. Ordinary profit, the line in the charts above, is a Japanese GAAP stage between operating profit and net income: operating profit plus non-operating income minus non-operating expenses. It does not exist under IFRS or US GAAP, and it can shrink while operating profit holds. The impairment here is booked below the operating line as an extraordinary loss, so a net loss can appear in a year when operating profit rises. Disclosures of this kind are published in Japanese only, and the store-level detail behind the impairment is not disclosed at all, so the consolidated figures are worth reconciling against the primary documents.

Closing

The word "loss" carries a lot of weight.

But whether the substance is an impairment or a weak core business changes how you read it.

When you see an earnings headline, it is worth looking once at what sits between operating profit and net income.
