Toyota Motor (7203 JP): net income up 26.1%, well above the 9.2% rise in operating profit

The gap comes from lines below operating profit, a lighter tax charge and a 7.7% smaller share count

Toyota Motor's results for April to September 2026 showed net income up 26.1% on the same period a year earlier.

Operating profit, the profit from the core business, rose 9.2%. The growth in net income is more than double that rate.

This post traces where that gap comes from, and how it connects to the buyback increase the company announced on the same day, using the interim results filing and the results presentation.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures, from earnings filings to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • Net income rose 26.1%, far above the 9.2% increase in operating profit.
  • Of the JPY 476.2bn increase in pre-tax income, the rise in operating profit accounted for JPY 185.4bn, or about 39%.
  • The average share count fell 7.7%, which lifted net income per share to a 36.7% increase.
  • The company raised its full-year operating profit forecast and added JPY 300bn to its buyback authorisation. Even after the raise, the full-year net income forecast is 7.2% below the prior year.

1. Why the growth rate rises at each step down the income statement

Toyota Motor (https://global.toyota/) is one of Japan's largest manufacturers, building and selling cars around the world.

On November 5, 2026, it reported results for the second quarter (April to September 2026) of the fiscal year ending March 2027.

The interim results filing, the document in which a company sets out the key figures of its results, lists the growth rate for each profit line.

Operating profit was JPY 2,191.1bn, up 9.2% on the same period a year earlier.

Operating profit is what the company earns from its core business, mainly selling cars and providing financial services.

Below it, pre-tax interim income was JPY 2,954.4bn, up 19.2%.

That line is operating profit plus or minus the share of profit of equity-method investments (the investor's share of the profit of companies it holds a stake in) and finance-related gains and losses.

Interim net income after tax was JPY 2,305.5bn, up 24.9%.

Of that, the part attributable to shareholders of Toyota, the parent company, was JPY 2,236.4bn, up 26.1%.

This post calls that figure net income.

Net income per share rose from JPY 136.07 to JPY 185.97, an increase of 36.7%.

As the chart shows, the growth rate gets larger the further down the statement you go.

Operating profit alone cannot explain the rise in net income.

2. Why lines other than operating profit made up most of the pre-tax increase

Pre-tax income rose JPY 476.2bn on the prior year.

Operating profit contributed JPY 185.4bn, about 39% of the total.

The rest came from increases in lines below operating profit.

The share of profit of equity-method investments rose JPY 85.7bn.

Other finance income rose from JPY 292.7bn to JPY 1,007.9bn, an increase of JPY 715.1bn.

Other finance costs, on the other hand, rose from JPY 25.0bn to JPY 492.5bn, an increase of JPY 467.4bn.

The filing says the other finance costs include a loss of JPY 95.4bn related to the deconsolidation of Hino Motors.

Neither the filing nor the presentation explains in detail what sits inside finance income and finance costs.

Foreign exchange gains and losses fell by JPY 92.6bn, and other gains and losses rose by JPY 50.0bn.

Together, the lines below operating profit lifted pre-tax income by JPY 290.8bn.

The tax burden also became lighter.

Corporate income tax expense divided by pre-tax income comes to 25.5% in the prior year and 22.0% this time.

That is the author's own division of the figures in the filing; the company does not say why the rate fell.

Operating profit itself was also driven more by currency and similar factors than by an improvement in the core business.

In the presentation's bridge of changes, the effect of exchange rates is a positive JPY 685.0bn.

The exchange rate assumption moved from JPY 146 to JPY 159 per dollar, 13 yen weaker.

The materials state that operating profit, excluding the effect of foreign exchange and swaps, changed by minus JPY 195.0bn.

In other words, the 9.2% rise in operating profit corresponds to a decline once the effect of foreign exchange and swaps is excluded.

The 26.1% rise in net income is the result of the operating profit increase combined with the lines below operating profit and the lighter tax charge.

3. How far did the smaller share count lift per-share profit?

Net income rose 26.1%, but net income per share rose 36.7%.

The difference comes from the denominator: the share count fell.

The weighted average number of shares in the interim period fell from 13,033.16m shares to 12,025.80m shares, a decline of 7.7%.

Divide a 26.1% increase in net income by a share count 7.7% smaller and you get 36.7%.

The share count fell because of buybacks.

In the cash flow statement, payments for purchases of treasury shares grew from JPY 40.0bn in the same period a year earlier to JPY 3,851.9bn.

The filing does not break down that JPY 3,851.9bn.

The presentation notes that the prior year's (fiscal year ended March 2026) authorisation of JPY 3,656.8bn included shares purchased through the tender offer connected with taking Toyota Industries private.

Whether the interim period's spending includes that tender offer cannot be confirmed from the materials alone.

For that reason, this post avoids reading the JPY 3,851.9bn as the size of "ordinary buybacks".

What can be confirmed is that the share count fell and lifted per-share profit, and that cash fell.

Cash and cash equivalents fell from JPY 12,659.6bn at the start of the period to JPY 9,484.4bn, a decrease of JPY 3,175.1bn.

4. How the company explained the upgrade and the larger authorisation

On the same day as the results, the company also announced its full-year outlook and shareholder returns.

The full-year operating profit forecast was raised from JPY 3,400bn to JPY 3,900bn, an increase of JPY 500bn.

That is above the prior year's actual JPY 3,766.2bn.

In the presentation, the company cites the accumulation of improvement efforts, such as a lighter tariff burden and growth in value chain earnings.

The full-year net income forecast also rose, from JPY 3,250bn to JPY 3,570bn, but it is still 7.2% below the prior year's JPY 3,848.0bn.

On buybacks, the company added JPY 300bn to the authorisation, taking this year's ceiling to JPY 1,300bn.

The presentation gives this reason for the increase:

Increase the acquisition limit by JPY 300bn to respond to requests from shareholders to sell their shares

--- Toyota Motor, "FY2027 Second Quarter Financial Results" presentation (November 5, 2026), translated from Japanese

The cancellation of treasury shares (the procedure that extinguishes shares the company holds) will also be increased by 1bn shares, bringing the cumulative total for this year to 1.2bn shares.

The materials put that 1.2bn shares at about JPY 3,500bn equivalent, at the market price as of the end of September 2026.

The company describes these moves as lowering its level of treasury shares and pursuing a capital policy that is mindful of capital efficiency.

The dividend is JPY 50 per share for the interim (up JPY 5 on the prior year), and the full-year forecast is unchanged at JPY 100 (also up JPY 5).

On the dividend, the company writes that it follows a policy of stable increases that rewards long-term shareholders.

5. Can this be read as a declaration of returning profit to shareholders?

Line up the facts so far and one reading becomes possible.

Operating profit, excluding the effect of foreign exchange and swaps, declined.

The full-year net income forecast is also below the prior year.

Within that, the company raised its operating profit forecast, widened the authorisation and kept a dividend forecast JPY 5 higher than the prior year.

It can be read as an announcement that puts the stance of returning profit to shareholders at the front, in a year when full-year net income is expected to fall short of the prior year.

This year's authorisation ceiling of JPY 1,300bn equals about 36% of the JPY 3,570bn full-year net income forecast.

That reading has limits.

The authorisation is a ceiling, not an amount actually bought.

The reason the company itself gives for the increase is "to respond to requests from shareholders to sell their shares".

It does not say "to return profit", so the word declaration is the author's interpretation.

The 26.1% rise in net income also does not directly reflect the momentum of the core business.

Lines below operating profit and the lighter tax charge pushed that growth rate up.

The second-half outlook, obtained by subtracting the interim actual from the full-year forecast, was set out in an earlier post.

Why it matters for global investors

Toyota's fiscal year ends on March 31, so the period discussed here is the first half (April to September) of the fiscal year ending March 2027. A Japanese income statement carries a block of lines between operating profit and pre-tax income — here the share of profit of equity-method investments, other finance income and costs, and foreign exchange gains and losses — and that block is where the gap in this article opens up. Toyota publishes an English version of this presentation, but most Japanese companies file their earnings statements and annual securities reports in Japanese only, on EDINET, Japan's FSA filing system (the counterpart to SEC EDGAR). Two further points are worth keeping in mind: a buyback authorisation is a ceiling rather than an amount actually bought, and cancelling treasury shares is a standard part of Japanese capital policy.

Closing

A headline saying that profit rose does not tell you which profit line rose, and the content changes a lot depending on the answer.

At Toyota this time, the operating profit increase accounted for about four-tenths of the pre-tax increase, and the rest came from lines below operating profit.

On top of that, the smaller share count pushed net income per share to a 36.7% increase.

Next time you look at a set of results, reading operating profit, net income and net income per share side by side shows where the increase came from.

How much of the authorisation is actually used can be checked in future disclosures.
