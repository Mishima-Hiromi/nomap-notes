MUFG (8306 JP): dividend per share up 3.1x in four years

A Japanese megabank group, read straight from its EDINET filings

A bank collects deposits and lends them out. Almost everyone has an account with one.

One group in that business, Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group (8306), reported net income of JPY 2,427.2bn for the fiscal year ended March 2026, and raised its dividend per share to JPY 86.

That is about 3.1x the JPY 28 of FY2022/3, four years earlier.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

What the company does

Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group is a financial group built around banking, trust banking and securities, and also covering credit cards and consumer lending, leasing, and asset management.

It has 345 consolidated subsidiaries and 55 affiliates accounted for using the equity method.

It is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime Market.

Business model: where the money comes from

The base of a bank's earnings is the margin it earns by lending out and investing the money it raises through deposits and other funding.

At the end of FY2026/3, deposits were JPY 239,439.2bn and loans were JPY 133,799.4bn.

Of ordinary income of JPY 14,620.8bn, income from lending and investment came to JPY 8,723.9bn, and fee income to JPY 2,666.3bn.

The profit measure the company itself watches is consolidated gross business profit.

For FY2026/3 that was JPY 2,377.2bn: gross business profit of JPY 5,944.4bn minus operating expenses of JPY 3,567.2bn. It was up JPY 786.0bn from the prior year.

The company explains the increase by the earnings contribution of acquisitions overseas, better margins as yen interest rates rose, and higher fee income in Japan and abroad.

The group is organized into business groups. Operating net profit by business group was:

  • Corporate Banking: JPY 707.0bn
  • Global CIB: JPY 580.3bn
  • Corporate & Wealth Management: JPY 408.0bn
  • Global Commercial Banking: JPY 387.6bn
  • Retail & Digital: JPY 285.9bn
  • Asset Management & Investor Services: JPY 152.5bn
  • Global Markets: -JPY 35.5bn

The biggest earner is Corporate Banking, which handles lending to large companies.

Revenue by business group could not be obtained from EDINET DB.

All of the business group figures are operating net profit, which is a different measure from group profit.

There is also profit coming from outside the core business.

Share of profit of entities accounted for using the equity method was JPY 845.5bn, up JPY 248.5bn from the prior year.

The company gives strong results at Morgan Stanley as the main reason for the increase.

The value it provides

Banking, trust banking, securities, cards and consumer lending, leasing and asset management are all delivered by one group.

What published numbers can confirm is scale.

It takes in JPY 239,439.2bn of deposits and lends out JPY 133,799.4bn.

Four of the business groups face companies rather than individuals: Corporate Banking, Global CIB, Corporate & Wealth Management, and Global Commercial Banking.

Together their operating net profit is JPY 2,082.9bn, about four-fifths of the JPY 2,485.8bn total for all seven groups.

Strengths that cannot be measured in numbers, such as how long relationships last or how hard it is to switch banks, are not covered here.

Basic data

Here are Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group's (8306) figures for FY2026/3. The same items appear each time.

  • Revenue: JPY 14,620.8bn (ordinary income)
  • Operating profit: no data
  • Operating margin: no data
  • Net income: JPY 2,427.2bn
  • ROE: 11.3%
  • Equity ratio: 5.2%
  • Dividend per share: JPY 86

Source: EDINET DB, annual securities report, FY2026/3.

Notable facts

Dividend per share up 3.1x in four years

Dividend per share went from JPY 28 in FY2022/3 to JPY 32, JPY 41 and JPY 64, and reached JPY 86 in FY2026/3.

The JPY 86 is an interim dividend of JPY 35 plus a year-end dividend of JPY 51. Total dividends were JPY 976.0bn, and the payout ratio was 40.3%.

Behind the increase are net income of JPY 2,427.2bn in FY2026/3, up 30.3% from the prior year, and a smaller number of shares. The payout ratio also rose, from about 32% in FY2022/3 (JPY 28 divided by EPS of JPY 88.44) to 40.3%.

For FY2027/3 the company forecasts a dividend of JPY 96, an interim JPY 48 plus a year-end JPY 48.

Shares outstanding fell by about 1.4bn

Shares outstanding went from 13.28bn in FY2022/3 to 12.69bn, 12.34bn and 12.07bn, and stood at 11.87bn in FY2026/3.

In FY2026/3 the company spent JPY 500.0bn buying back its own shares and JPY 290.4bn cancelling them.

Fewer shares mean higher earnings per share on the same net income.

Earnings per share rose from JPY 160.02 in the prior year to JPY 213.17 in FY2026/3.

Global Markets is still in the red

Operating net profit of Global Markets was a loss of JPY 35.5bn.

The gap with the prior year was an improvement of JPY 622.4bn, which means the prior year was a loss of about JPY 657.9bn.

The earnings release does not explain the improvement by business group. In the consolidated explanation, it says other business profit rose on the reversal of the sale losses booked when the bond portfolio was restructured the year before.

Of the seven business groups, Global Markets is the only one in the red.

Points to watch as an investor

The condition for growth is that better margins from higher yen interest rates continue, and that fee income and acquisitions overseas keep adding to profit.

The company's net income target for FY2027/3 is JPY 2,700.0bn.

Net income in the first quarter of FY2027/3 (April to June) was JPY 809.4bn, up 48.2% from JPY 546.0bn a year earlier.

ROE is 11.3%, net income divided by shareholders' equity.

The condition for a break is deterioration in credit costs or in the valuation of securities.

Total credit-related costs in FY2026/3 were JPY 355.8bn, up JPY 247.1bn from the prior year.

Gains and losses on stocks and other securities were JPY 486.0bn, down JPY 106.5bn, mainly on the reversal of large gains on sales in the prior year.

An equity ratio of 5.2% looks low.

That is because a bank raises money through deposits, a liability, and lends it out, and because total assets are large at JPY 431,000bn.

So against the level of profit, you need to check what a loss would do.

Operating cash flow was minus JPY 23,064.4bn in FY2026/3.

At a bank, increases and decreases in loans and deposits run through this line, so it cannot be read the same way as at an ordinary operating company.

Banks do not disclose operating profit, so profit is read through gross business profit and net income.

Three things to confirm in the next earnings report: whether net income is moving toward the JPY 2,700.0bn target, whether credit-related costs are rising, and whether the Global Markets loss is shrinking.

Why it matters for global investors

MUFG's disclosures sit on EDINET, and the underlying documents are mostly published only in Japanese, so the standard toolkit does not transfer cleanly. Banks in Japan do not disclose operating profit, which is why this post reads profit through consolidated gross business profit and net income instead. The 5.2% equity ratio and the negative operating cash flow look alarming by industrial-company standards, but both follow from the balance sheet of a bank that funds lending with deposits and carries JPY 431,000bn of total assets. Read the same statements with those conventions in mind and the numbers line up.

Closing

A net income figure above JPY 2tn catches the eye, but the 3.1x rise in the dividend came from three things: higher profit, fewer shares, and a higher payout ratio.

Bank figures include many items, such as the equity ratio and cash flow, that are read differently from those of an ordinary operating company.

Once those differences are clear, the meaning of the numbers in the earnings report comes into view.
