Sony Group (6758 JP): forecast raised by JPY 500bn in sales and JPY 150bn in profit

The added profit equals 30% of the added sales, but only three segments grew sales at all

Sony Group raised its full-year earnings forecast on November 5, 2026.

The headline numbers are large: JPY 13,000bn in sales, JPY 1,870bn in operating profit.

Divide the added profit by the added sales, though, and the figures read a little differently.

This post splits the revision by segment and checks what is inside it.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures, from earnings materials to annual securities reports on EDINET, the financial regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • Sony Group raised its full-year sales forecast by JPY 500bn and its operating profit forecast by JPY 150bn, compared with the figures given in July.
  • The added operating profit equals 30% of the added sales. That is more than twice the 13.8% operating margin in the July forecast.
  • Inside the three segments that raised sales (Games, Music and Pictures), the added profit is JPY 115bn on JPY 500bn of added sales, which is 23%.
  • Of the remaining JPY 35bn of added profit, JPY 30bn sits in image sensors, where sales are almost flat and the gain came from lower costs and a favourable exchange rate. The breakdown of the last JPY 5bn could not be identified.
  • First-half operating profit already reaches about 59% of the full-year forecast, so the second half is assumed to run at a lower margin than the first.

1. What changed in the full-year forecast between July and November

Sony Group (https://www.sony.com/ja/) runs games such as PlayStation, music, movies, image sensors, and electronics including televisions and cameras.

Fiscal 2026 is the year ending March 2027, and on November 5, 2026 the company reported its half-year results for April to September.

Alongside those results it revised its forecast for the full year.

Operating profit is the profit earned by the core business. Operating profit divided by sales is the operating margin, which tells you how many yen of core profit come from each JPY 100 of sales.

Cross-checking AV Watch's report (AV Watch is an AV equipment news site run by Impress) against the earnings release data in EDINET DB (a database that structures disclosures filed on EDINET, the Financial Services Agency's filing system), the forecast moved as follows.

Sales go from JPY 12,500bn to JPY 13,000bn, and operating profit from JPY 1,720bn to JPY 1,870bn.

The increases are JPY 500bn in sales and JPY 150bn in operating profit.

The net income forecast is JPY 1,330bn.

The operating margin is 13.8% in the July forecast and 14.4% in the current one.

In the prior year (the fiscal year ended March 2026), sales were JPY 12,479.6bn and operating profit was JPY 1,447.5bn.

The new forecast is about 29% above the prior year in operating profit.

This is the second raise this fiscal year.

The initial forecast in May 2026 was JPY 12,300bn in sales and JPY 1,600bn in operating profit.

In July it went to JPY 12,500bn and JPY 1,720bn, and this time it went higher again.

Since the initial forecast, sales have been lifted by JPY 700bn and operating profit by JPY 270bn.

Now divide the added operating profit by the added sales.

JPY 150bn divided by JPY 500bn is 30%.

Compare that 30% with the 13.8% operating margin the company forecast in July.

Every JPY 100 of added sales carries JPY 30 of profit, more than twice the company-wide average of about JPY 14.

Part of the added sales is turning into profit at a higher rate than the company average.

So which segments produced that 30%?

2. How the added profit differs between segments that grew sales and those that did not

According to AV Watch, three segments raised their sales forecast in this revision.

Game & Network Services went up JPY 290bn to JPY 4,830bn, Music by JPY 110bn to JPY 2,300bn, and Pictures by JPY 100bn to JPY 1,760bn.

The three together come to JPY 500bn, the same as the company-wide increase.

Operating profit forecasts rose by JPY 40bn to JPY 700bn in Games, by JPY 25bn to JPY 445bn in Music, and by JPY 50bn to JPY 200bn in Pictures.

The three segments add JPY 115bn of operating profit, which is 23% of the JPY 500bn of added sales.

Segment by segment, the rates differ widely.

Games adds JPY 40bn of profit on JPY 290bn of sales, which is 13.8%.

That stays at the same level as the company-wide average in the July forecast.

Music adds JPY 25bn on JPY 110bn, which is 22.7%.

Pictures adds JPY 50bn on JPY 100bn, exactly 50%.

Games accounts for 58% of the added sales but 27% of the company's JPY 150bn of added profit.

Pictures accounts for 20% of the added sales and 33% of the added profit.

Where sales grow and where profit grows do not match.

The company's explanations differ by segment too.

AV Watch reports that the Games raise reflects "higher sales of game software other than self-produced titles and cost improvements", among other factors.

In Music, the reasons are higher income from live performances and merchandise, and higher income from music streaming.

In Pictures, theatrical revenue from "Spider-Man: Brand New Day" rose in the July to September quarter, adding JPY 167.7bn to sales.

There is added profit outside those three segments as well.

In Imaging & Sensing Solutions (image sensors, including those for smartphones), the sales forecast is almost flat at JPY 2,120bn.

Even so, its operating profit forecast was raised by JPY 30bn to JPY 450bn.

The reason reported is "lower costs and a favourable exchange rate".

The full-year forecast for Entertainment, Technology & Services (televisions, cameras and similar) is unchanged from July.

Adding up the segment increases so far gives JPY 115bn (three segments) plus JPY 30bn (sensors), or JPY 145bn.

The gap to the company-wide JPY 150bn is JPY 5bn.

The breakdown of that JPY 5bn was not visible in the materials checked.

The next figure lines up the segment increases in operating profit.

In the segments that grew sales, the added profit rate is 23%, below the 30% for the company as a whole.

The 7-point gap, or JPY 35bn, is the JPY 30bn from image sensors, where profit rose without sales, plus the unidentified JPY 5bn.

So it cannot simply be said that "JPY 500bn of added sales produced JPY 150bn of added profit".

The three segments that grew sales added JPY 115bn of profit; of the remaining JPY 35bn, JPY 30bn came from lower costs and the exchange rate in image sensors, and JPY 5bn is unidentified.

Note also that the three segments' increase includes "cost improvements" in Games, so not all of the JPY 115bn comes from higher sales either.

3. After earning close to six-tenths of the forecast in the first half, what does the second half assume?

Next, split the full-year forecast into first and second half.

In the earnings release data in EDINET DB, April to September sales were JPY 6,267.2bn, up 9.4% on the same period a year earlier, and operating profit was JPY 1,098.8bn, up 42.9%.

That operating profit is about 59% of the full-year forecast of JPY 1,870bn.

Subtracting the first half from the full-year forecast leaves JPY 771.2bn of operating profit and JPY 6,732.8bn of sales for the second half.

The operating margin works out at 17.5% in the first half and 11.5% in the second half.

Those second-half figures come from subtracting the first half from the full-year forecast; the company has not presented second-half figures of its own.

Why the second half is assumed to carry a lower margin could not be read from the articles checked.

The company, for its part, acknowledges that unusual items are mixed into the first-half profit.

Chief Financial Officer Lin Tao said at the briefing, as reported:

there were also gains booked from tariff refunds and from some demand being pulled forward, but we were able to exceed JPY 1trn in half-year consolidated operating profit for the first time

--- from AV Watch's report of the fiscal 2026 second-quarter results briefing

A tariff refund means tariffs paid in the United States being returned.

AV Watch reports that the rise in operating profit in Entertainment, Technology & Services came from that refund.

Operating profit in that segment for July to September was JPY 64.1bn, up JPY 3.2bn.

Conversely, the full-year forecast also includes items that push profit down.

For the Kumamoto Technology Center, damaged by the Kumamoto earthquake on July 28, the company says it has reflected an annual loss of about JPY 16bn in the current outlook.

At Sony Honda Mobility (a joint venture between Sony and Honda that stopped developing and selling the AFEELA electric vehicle and decided in April 2026 to scale the business down), an additional loss of about JPY 30bn was booked in the July to September quarter.

Tao said the loss from scaling the business down is "largely complete".

In other words, the full-year forecast contains both temporary items that lift profit and temporary items that weigh on it.

No figures splitting the JPY 150bn into temporary and recurring parts appear in the materials checked.

That is why the 30% rate should not be read as a direct guide to how much profit the next increase in sales would bring.

Why it matters for global investors

Japanese companies close their books on March 31, so fiscal 2026 here runs from April 2026 to March 2027, and the first half is April to September; this is why the forecast tables show a "second half" running from October to March. The segment-level changes come from the Japanese-language earnings release and the Japanese trade press reporting on it, and the segment increases do not have to add up to the consolidated increase: here JPY 5bn of the JPY 150bn is not broken out in the disclosures checked. Guidance is also given as a full-year figure, so any second-half number is a subtraction by the reader rather than company guidance. The margin and profit figures for both halves should be read with that in mind.

Closing

A raised full-year forecast does not tell you what is inside it from the size of the number alone.

At Sony, the three segments that grew sales added JPY 115bn of profit, which is 23% of the JPY 500bn of added sales. Of the JPY 35bn gap to the company-wide JPY 150bn, JPY 30bn is lower costs and the exchange rate in image sensors, and JPY 5bn is unidentified.

Next time you look at results, check whether the segments that grew sales are the same ones that grew profit.

The size of the sales increase and the size of the profit increase are worth checking separately.
