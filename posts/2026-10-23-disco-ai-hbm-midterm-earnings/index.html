DISCO (6146 JP): operating profit rose 47.9%, outpacing 32.1% revenue growth

A Japanese chip-equipment maker's first half, read from its own Japanese-language disclosures

Semiconductor equipment is a business where revenue swings with how willing customers are to invest.

If personnel and research costs swell as fast as sales, little of that growth is left as profit.

DISCO's first half went the other way: operating profit grew faster than revenue.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from Japanese-language earnings releases on TDnet to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • For the six months to September 2026, DISCO reported revenue of JPY 257.06bn (up 32.1% year on year) and operating profit of JPY 116.64bn (up 47.9%). Demand for leading-edge semiconductors stayed high, and margins rose.
  • The operating margin was 45.4%, up from 40.5% a year earlier.
  • Gross margin improved from 68.9% to 71.5%, and selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses grew 22.0%, slower than the 37.2% growth in gross profit.
  • Subtracting the first half from the April-December plan leaves third-quarter revenue of JPY 132.4bn and operating profit of JPY 58.1bn, below the second quarter. Shipments, however, are planned at JPY 149.6bn, above the second quarter's JPY 148.6bn.
  • The interim dividend is JPY 186 per share, above last year's JPY 129 and the most recent forecast of JPY 171.

1. First-half results beside last year

What DISCO makes

DISCO makes dicing saws that cut semiconductor wafers, grinders that thin them, and precision processing tools used in cutting and grinding.

The shares carry securities code 6146 and are listed on the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

Company website: https://www.disco.co.jp/

Demand for generative AI has kept investment in data centers running.

Demand for high-performance semiconductors such as leading-edge logic and HBM (high-bandwidth memory) was at a high level.

HBM is high-speed memory used in AI chips.

Revenue and operating profit growth

In the first-half results published on October 22, 2026, revenue came to JPY 257.06bn and operating profit to JPY 116.64bn.

Revenue rose 32.1% from a year earlier, and operating profit rose 47.9%.

Net income was JPY 80.37bn, up 43.7%.

The operating margin was 45.4%, against 40.5% a year earlier.

Below, the quarters are 1Q (April-June), 2Q (July-September) and 3Q (October-December).

From 1Q to 2Q, both revenue and operating profit rose sharply.

1Q revenue was JPY 114.3bn and operating profit JPY 49.0bn.

2Q revenue was JPY 142.8bn and operating profit JPY 67.6bn.

2Q revenue rose 24.9% from 1Q, and operating profit rose 37.9%.

The 2Q operating margin was 47.4%, up from 42.9% in 1Q.

The company's own July guidance for the first half was revenue of JPY 242.8bn and operating profit of JPY 104.9bn.

The actual figures beat that guidance.

2. Gross margin and SG&A lifted the operating margin

Operating profit grew faster than revenue because gross margin rose and SG&A expenses grew more slowly than gross profit.

First-half gross profit was JPY 183.92bn, up 37.2% from a year earlier.

That is faster than the 32.1% growth in revenue.

The gross margin was 71.5%, up 2.6 points from 68.9% a year earlier.

The company cites foreign exchange effects and an increase in higher value-added products.

SG&A expenses were JPY 67.28bn, up 22.0%.

The increase came from personnel and research and development costs.

Even so, gross profit rose by JPY 49.91bn, more than the JPY 12.14bn increase in SG&A.

As a result, operating profit rose by JPY 37.77bn.

In 2Q alone, the gross margin was 71.7%, above 69.5% a year earlier.

The company's presentation says the gross margin rose mainly on precision processing tools.

Part of that improvement, however, includes a foreign exchange benefit.

The company itself describes it as "foreign exchange effects and an increase in higher value-added products," and the materials do not break out each contribution.

A weaker yen is a tailwind while it lasts, but a stronger yen weakens that tailwind.

3. Why the April-December plan puts 3Q below 2Q

On October 22, the company published consolidated guidance for the nine months from April to December, which it had not disclosed before.

This is the first such disclosure, so there is no previous forecast to compare with.

Revenue is put at JPY 389.5bn (up 28.2% year on year), operating profit at JPY 174.7bn (up 38.4%) and net income at JPY 121.9bn (up 31.6%).

Simply subtracting the first-half actuals from those figures gives the 3Q plan.

Revenue is JPY 132.4bn and operating profit JPY 58.1bn.

Both are below 2Q revenue of JPY 142.8bn and operating profit of JPY 67.6bn.

They are above the year-earlier 3Q, however, when revenue was JPY 109.3bn and operating profit JPY 47.3bn.

The figures in the presentation are rounded to the nearest JPY 100m.

The company expects 3Q shipments of JPY 149.6bn.

That is more than the JPY 148.6bn in 2Q, so shipments are not falling.

Revenue falling below 2Q is thought to reflect a shift in the timing of customer acceptance.

Acceptance means the customer has received the delivered equipment and finished inspecting it.

DISCO books revenue at that point.

In 2Q, acceptance progressed and revenue rose 24.9% from 1Q.

Shipments rose 9%, so revenue grew faster than shipments.

In 3Q the pattern reverses: shipments are planned above 2Q while revenue is planned below it.

The plan reads as if acceptance pulled forward into 2Q, leaving 3Q revenue relatively low.

The 3Q shipment outlook by product has grinders up 10% from 2Q, blade dicers down 5% and laser saws flat.

Precision processing equipment overall is planned up 5%, while precision processing tools are planned down 5%.

4. The gap between shipments and revenue

First-half shipments were JPY 284.58bn, up 37.2% from a year earlier.

The company says first-half shipments set a record high.

1Q shipments were JPY 135.9bn.

2Q shipments alone were JPY 148.6bn, also a record.

The 3Q shipment plan is JPY 149.6bn, above 2Q.

The April-December shipment forecast is JPY 434.2bn.

The company explains the rise in machinery revenue through the progress of acceptance. Equipment shipments are counted at an earlier stage than that acceptance.

So shipments running at a high level can be read as a sign that demand has not suddenly thinned.

In 2Q dicer shipments, IC applications accounted for 73%.

On top of IC demand led by generative AI, shipments for optical semiconductors and optical components related to CPO (co-packaged optics) are also increasing.

CPO puts optical communication components in the same package as a semiconductor.

If acceptance is delayed further, however, 3Q revenue will fall short of the plan.

Shipments in line with the plan do not guarantee the same amount of revenue.

That is the first thing to check in the next results.

5. The foreign exchange tailwind and acceptance timing

It is too early to treat the entire margin improvement as underlying strength.

The company assumes an exchange rate of JPY 151 per US dollar for 3Q.

Its foreign exchange sensitivity is about JPY 2.1bn of annual operating profit per yen.

A one-yen move toward a weaker yen adds about JPY 2.1bn to operating profit, and a stronger yen does the reverse.

Foreign exchange also contributed to the first-half gross margin improvement, so a turn toward a stronger yen would weaken the margin tailwind.

The second factor is product mix.

In 3Q, shipments of precision processing tools are planned down 5% from 2Q.

Tools are the product the company names as the center of the gross margin increase.

A lower mix can therefore be a headwind for margins.

The 3Q operating margin plan is 43.9%, below 2Q's 47.4%.

That figure reads as lower revenue, a change in product mix and the exchange-rate assumption all combined.

What to watch next is how far 3Q acceptance progresses.

Even if shipments exceed JPY 149.6bn, revenue will not grow if acceptance is late.

Conversely, if acceptance moves ahead, 3Q revenue could exceed the plan.

6. The JPY 186 interim dividend and cash on hand

First-half operating cash flow was an inflow of JPY 111.71bn, up 181.5% from a year earlier.

Investing cash flow was an outflow of JPY 54.81bn.

Free cash flow, the difference, was a JPY 56.89bn increase.

Cash and cash equivalents at the end of September were JPY 200.63bn.

Contract liabilities were JPY 79.62bn, up from JPY 50.01bn at the end of March.

Contract liabilities are money received from customers before revenue is booked.

They can be read as prepayments for future revenue piling up.

The interim dividend is JPY 186 per share.

That is above the most recent forecast of JPY 171 and up from JPY 129 a year earlier.

The total dividend is JPY 20.18bn. The company set the interim dividend by applying a 25% payout ratio to first-half consolidated net income.

The year-end dividend has not been decided.

The company's policy is that if it is not in loss at the fiscal year-end and cash after dividend and corporate tax payments exceeds planned funding needs, it will pay an additional dividend of about one-third of the excess.

Why it matters for global investors

DISCO's shares trade on the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange, and this update first arrived as a Japanese-language TDnet release plus a Japanese-language presentation; the company's investor relations pages are also published in English. The fiscal year ends in March, so the "first half" here runs from April to September, not January to June, and the nine-month plan covers April to December. Japanese companies also file annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's disclosure system, and those reports are published in Japanese.

Closing

DISCO's first half beat the prior year not only on revenue but on margins.

The 3Q plan alone looks like a slowdown, but shipments are planned above 2Q's record.

Reading it as the timing of acceptance and the exchange-rate assumption, rather than as the end of demand, is more useful for the next decision.
