Fast Retailing (9983 JP): Europe and North America overtake Greater China

The profit engine moved west in FY2026, while net income guidance points to a slower year

When people talk about UNIQLO's earnings, the question tends to be how much it sold in China.

But in the results for the fiscal year ended August 2026, published on October 8, the centre of growth is somewhere else.

Here are the regional numbers, side by side.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from Japanese-language earnings releases to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • UNIQLO's profit engine is shifting from Greater China to Europe and North America.
  • North America and Europe together posted revenue of JPY 877.5bn, above Greater China's JPY 724.0bn. The company says the West overtook China for the first time.
  • Business profit grew 69.0% year on year in Europe, 53.3% in North America and 24.6% in Greater China.
  • For the fiscal year ending August 2027, the company forecasts net income growth of only 3.2%. The leading region has changed, but profit growth is expected to settle a step lower.

What kind of company is Fast Retailing

Fast Retailing (securities code 9983) is the apparel company that operates UNIQLO and GU.

In its earnings release it reports four businesses: UNIQLO Japan, UNIQLO International, GU and Global Brands.

Company website: https://www.fastretailing.com/jp/

1. A fifth consecutive record year

First, the whole picture.

Revenue was JPY 3.9633tn, up 16.6% year on year.

Business profit, the company's measure of profit at the business level, was JPY 718.4bn, up 30.4%.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was JPY 542.5bn, up 25.3%.

The company says this was a record result for the fifth consecutive year.

The subject of this post, though, is not the strength of the whole.

It is which region pulled that strength along.

2. Overseas UNIQLO earns more than twice the domestic business

By business, the gap is clear.

UNIQLO Japan posted revenue of JPY 1,084.8bn, up 5.7%.

UNIQLO International posted revenue of JPY 2,411.1bn, up 26.2%.

Business profit at UNIQLO International was JPY 439.8bn, up 44.1%.

Business profit at UNIQLO Japan was JPY 196.0bn, up 8.1%.

Overseas revenue is more than twice the domestic figure.

On growth rates too, the overseas business is far ahead of Japan.

So within that overseas business, where is the growth coming from?

3. North America and Europe together overtook Greater China for the first time

Here is UNIQLO International split by region.

Greater China (mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan) posted revenue of JPY 724.0bn.

South Korea, Southeast Asia, India and Australia came to JPY 809.4bn.

North America was JPY 364.9bn and Europe JPY 512.6bn.

North America plus Europe comes to JPY 877.5bn.

In the earnings release, the company writes that the sales scale in Europe and the US overtook both Greater China and the South Korea, Southeast Asia, India and Australia region for the first time.

Its wording is that the West has "grown into a thick pillar."

Greater China is not weak.

Greater China also grew revenue 11.3% and business profit 24.6%.

Looking at mainland China alone on a local-currency basis, revenue rose about 3% and business profit about 18%.

The company says structural reform of the business progressed and that it was a year with a sense of regaining growth.

In other words, Greater China is in the middle of a rebuild.

Alongside that, Europe and North America grew faster than it did.

4. Profit growth is larger in Europe and North America

Comparing growth rates in business profit makes the ranking even clearer.

Europe posted JPY 91.7bn, up 69.0%.

North America posted JPY 67.9bn, up 53.3%.

South Korea, Southeast Asia, India and Australia posted JPY 168.3bn, up 44.0%.

Greater China posted JPY 112.0bn, up 24.6%.

In absolute profit, the South Korea, Southeast Asia, India and Australia region is still the largest.

Its business profit margin is also the highest, at 20.8%.

North America was 18.6%, Europe 17.9% and Greater China 15.5%.

Europe and North America still sit above Greater China on margin.

What stands out is that they are stacking up revenue quickly while in that position.

The company explains that, on top of flagship store openings and brand messaging, new products that caught trends such as barrel pants and sweats brought in new customers.

One reading is that hit products are spreading across regions.

5. Next year's net income forecast is up 3.2%: a number that speaks to caution

Here is the company's forecast for the fiscal year ending August 2027.

Revenue is projected at JPY 4,450.0bn, up 12.3%.

Business profit is projected at JPY 830.0bn, up 15.5%.

Operating profit is projected at JPY 830.0bn, up 11.7%.

Profit before income taxes is projected at JPY 880.0bn, up 9.7%.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent is projected at JPY 560.0bn, up 3.2%.

The further down the profit line you go, the smaller the growth rate becomes.

Finance income and expenses were a positive JPY 59.3bn this fiscal year.

The breakdown is net interest of a positive JPY 52.2bn and foreign exchange gains of JPY 7.1bn.

In next year's forecast, the gap between profit before income taxes and operating profit is JPY 50.0bn.

That gap is calculated from the forecast figures; the company has not disclosed a breakdown.

In the outlook section of the earnings release, there was no explanation of why net income growth becomes smaller.

The assumed exchange rates are JPY 156.2 per US dollar, JPY 181.8 per euro and JPY 22.5 per Chinese yuan.

The dividend was JPY 850 a year for the fiscal year ended August 2026, a payout ratio of 48.1%.

The forecast for the fiscal year ending August 2027 is JPY 900 a year.

The company's outlook is that even with business momentum still strong, final profit growth will not match this year's.

The change in the regional mix points to where growth will come from next.

Looking only at next year's net income figure, though, the company's forecast is not uniformly bullish.

Why it matters for global investors

Fast Retailing reports under IFRS, and "business profit" is its own measure, defined as revenue minus cost of sales minus selling, general and administrative expenses, so it is not simply the IFRS operating profit line; for the fiscal year ending August 2027 the company forecasts both business profit and operating profit at JPY 830.0bn. Its fiscal year ends in August, so FY2026 here means the year ended 31 August 2026, with results published on 8 October 2026. "Greater China" in these disclosures is a regional grouping of mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan, not mainland China alone, and the mainland-only growth rate is given on a local-currency basis. The earnings release and annual securities report are published in Japanese, while the company also provides English IR pages, which is where the definition of business profit can be checked.

Closing

Reading UNIQLO's numbers, the era when one word, "China," could describe the whole thing appears to be ending.

Next time you look at its results, glance at the regional table once.

Knowing which region is growing changes how the same revenue figure reads.
