SOFT99 (4464): a filing with an unchanged 53.15% stake, and why it matters

One change report, one reference date, and the two-thirds needed for a squeeze-out

On September 17, Effissimo Capital Management filed a change report on SOFT99 Corporation (4464).

The holding ratio in it is 53.15%, exactly where it was in the previous filing. Not one percentage point moved.

Normally that is a document you skip.

But when you look at what was happening on the same reference date, the filing reads differently.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • The change report Effissimo filed on September 17 shows its 53.15% stake unchanged from the previous filing.
  • On the reference date, September 11, SOFT99 announced a share consolidation, the abolition of its unit-share provisions, a partial amendment to its articles of incorporation and a record date for an extraordinary shareholders meeting.
  • On the same September 11, Santrade filed a large shareholding report for 14.57% and stood alongside Effissimo as a joint holder.
  • 53.15% plus 14.57% is 67.72%, above the two-thirds required for a special resolution on the consolidation.
  • The report in which the ratio did not move is the one showing that the squeeze-out of minority shareholders has reached the execution stage.

1. Why a report with zero change in ratio matters

A change report is normally filed when a holding ratio moves by 1% or more.

In this filing, Effissimo's ratio is still 53.15%.

The stated reasons for the change are a change in important contracts such as security agreements, the addition of a joint holder, and an increase of 1% or more in the shareholding ratio.

Look only at the number and nothing appears to have happened.

The reference date of the report is September 11, 2026.

On that same day, SOFT99 announced a share consolidation, the abolition of the provisions on its unit share count, a partial amendment to its articles of incorporation, and a record date for an extraordinary shareholders meeting.

The cancellation of treasury shares was announced the same day.

So on the day the number did not move, the company had started procedures to reshape its shareholder base.

What SOFT99 Corporation does

It is a chemicals manufacturer headquartered in Osaka.

It makes and sells chemical products for cars and households, including the Glaco glass coating and the Fukupika body wax (company website: https://www.soft99.co.jp/).

What Effissimo Capital Management is

It is a Singapore-based fund that invests in Japanese equities.

It was founded in 2006 by people from the former Murakami Fund, and is known as an activist that makes active proposals to the companies it invests in.

2. When SOFT99 shares left a market with prices

Looking at the large shareholding and change reports of the past 18 months, every major movement in SOFT99 shares was off-market, and most were at a fixed JPY 4,100 per share.

KeePer Technical Laboratory bought shares at JPY 1,582 in March 2025 and sold the entire stake at JPY 4,100 eight months later, on November 13, 2025.

Both of Effissimo's purchases were at a flat JPY 4,100, with no movement of a single yen.

Over this period there is no trace of ordinary on-market trading setting a price.

What KeePer Technical Laboratory does

It is headquartered in Obu, Aichi Prefecture, and runs KeePer LABO, a chain of car-coating specialists (company website: https://keepergiken.co.jp/).

It appears here as both buyer and seller, but what it intended with this series of transactions cannot be read from the disclosed data.

3. From support for the tender offer to delisting

Laid out in order, Effissimo's involvement was never limited to buying shares; it moved together with decisions on the company side.

  • February 17, 2026: the SOFT99 board expressed support for the tender offer by ECM Master Fund SPV3 and recommended that shareholders tender.
  • February 20, 2026: the shareholder benefit program was abolished.
  • March 6, 2026: Effissimo's stake rose from 35.04% to 53.15%, an increase of 18.11 points.
  • March 9, 2026: the tender offer result was announced, and the parent company and other related companies changed.
  • May 15, 2026: the company disclosed, on the same day, its outlook for the procedures to take the shares private and its plan for meeting the listing maintenance standards, which put it into an improvement period.
  • July 1, 2026: it disclosed information on the financial statements of its non-listed parent and matters concerning its controlling shareholder.

The tender offer was run not by Effissimo itself but by an investment vehicle, ECM Master Fund SPV3.

In SOFT99's disclosures, SPV3 is positioned as an "other related company."

From the abolition of the shareholder benefit to the prospect of delisting and entry into the listing-standard improvement period, it all moved in one direction in a little over half a year.

4. What was announced on September 11

On September 11, 2026, SOFT99 announced the following on the same day.

A share consolidation and the abolition of the provisions on its unit share count, a partial amendment to its articles of incorporation, and a record date for an extraordinary shareholders meeting.

The cancellation of treasury shares.

And with the same September 11 as the reference date, Santrade filed a large shareholding report for 3,246,528 shares (14.57%).

A share consolidation is a procedure that combines shares so that fractions of less than one share arise.

Combined with the abolition of the unit-share provisions, it is used as a mechanism to cash out minority holdings as fractional shares, effectively ending their status as shareholders.

That is the standard procedure for a squeeze-out.

The change report of September 17 is therefore a document that mirrors the state of the company on the day that procedure began to move.

5. What 53.15% + 14.57% = 67.72% means

A share consolidation is a special resolution matter at a shareholders meeting, and passing it requires two-thirds or more of the votes.

Effissimo's 53.15% alone does not reach that level.

Add Santrade's 14.57%, filed with the same September 11 reference date, and the total is 67.72%.

That is above two-thirds (about 66.67%).

This is where the September 17 change report, with not one point of change, matters.

The ratio itself is static, but through the form of a joint holder, the two-thirds appears to be in place.

Three caveats are needed here.

First, 67.72% is a simple sum of the ratios in the large shareholding reports, and does not commit anyone to how votes will actually be cast at the meeting.

Second, the two-thirds for a special resolution is measured against the voting rights of shareholders present at the meeting, a different denominator from the ratio of shares outstanding.

Treasury share cancellation was also announced on September 11, so the number of shares outstanding will itself move from here.

The 67.72% therefore has to be read as a rough estimate as of today.

Finally, the evidence that Santrade is a joint holder of Effissimo goes only as far as the circumstance that the large shareholding report was filed with the same reference date, September 11, 2026.

The joint holder section of the filed document itself has not been checked for this article.

Why it matters for global investors

Japan's large shareholding reports and change reports are filed on EDINET, and most are published only in Japanese, so a filing like this one rarely reaches readers outside the country. The regime aggregates holdings through the concept of joint holders, which is why a stake can cross a threshold without a single fund buying more shares. And a special resolution is measured against the voting rights present at the meeting, not against shares outstanding, so the ratio in a filing and the ratio that decides a vote are not the same number. For a squeeze-out, that difference is the whole game.

Closing

This article followed one report in which the ratio did not move, and asked why it can still be called a move.

If you track only changes in the number, the September 17 document would have gone unnoticed.

The meaning is in the same reference date carrying both the consolidation announcement and another entity's large shareholding report.

If a follow-up filing appears, this article will be updated.
