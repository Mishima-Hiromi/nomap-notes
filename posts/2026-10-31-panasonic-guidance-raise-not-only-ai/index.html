Panasonic Holdings (6752 JP): guidance raised 20.3%, and AI was not the only reason

The biggest item behind second-quarter adjusted operating profit was price revisions and rationalisation, not volume

A headline saying guidance was raised on AI demand is easy to grasp, and it flattens the contents into one thing.

Panasonic Holdings raised its operating profit forecast for the current fiscal year on October 30, 2026.

The reasons were not AI alone.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from Japanese-language earnings releases to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • Panasonic HD raised its operating profit forecast for the fiscal year ending March 2027 from JPY 590bn to JPY 710bn, an increase of 20.3%.
  • Reuters attributes the raise to AI demand plus price changes, FX, and the disappearance of last year's US tariff impact.
  • In the second quarter (July to September), the largest item behind the rise in adjusted operating profit was not volume growth (JPY 60bn) but price revisions and rationalisation (JPY 74bn).
  • The amounts for FX and tariffs could not be found in the materials available. On what could be confirmed, the AI contribution cannot be separated out.

1. What the company raised, and by how much

Panasonic Holdings (securities code 6752) is the holding company of the Panasonic group, which makes home appliances, batteries, electronic components and other products.

Company website: https://holdings.panasonic/jp/ (Japanese)

In the interim earnings release it published on October 30, it revised up its consolidated forecast for the current fiscal year (April 2026 to March 2027).

An upward revision means the company lifts, partway through the year, the full-year outlook it had published itself.

Operating profit rose from JPY 590bn to JPY 710bn, an increase of JPY 120bn.

Adjusted operating profit, which the company shows alongside as a reference figure, went from JPY 650bn to JPY 780bn.

Adjusted operating profit is revenue minus cost of sales and selling, general and administrative expenses.

In the current forecast, it sits JPY 70bn above operating profit.

What makes up that gap was not written in the materials available.

The earnings release only lists the revised numbers and gives no reasons.

The reasons were given at the briefing held the same day.

President Yuki Kusumi looked back and said the company needed to deliver results from last year's structural reform, and added that AI-related demand expanded more than assumed, which also pushed earnings up.

Mynavi News reports that the factors behind the profit increase include demand expansion in AI infrastructure and elsewhere, plus the effects of fixed-cost cuts through structural reform, price revisions and rationalisation.

In other words, the company's own explanation includes factors other than AI.

Read only as "raised on AI demand", the story drops structural reform and price revisions out of the picture.

2. How far the AI contribution can be confirmed

The part where AI's contribution can be followed in numbers is in fact limited.

According to reports on the briefing, the AI-related business in Industry (the segment handling capacitors and substrate materials) raised its revenue outlook for the year from JPY 310bn to JPY 360bn.

The increase is JPY 50bn.

The total revenue forecast, meanwhile, was raised by JPY 600bn.

The AI-related addition is one-twelfth of that.

The breakdown of the remaining JPY 550bn is not known from the materials available.

For the data-centre storage systems in Energy (the battery segment), the briefing said "strong growth in line with the initial plan continues".

Second-quarter revenue was 1.6x the prior year, and the full year is expected to be 1.7x.

The growth is large, but because it is described as in line with the plan set at the start of the year, it is not the part that counts as the upside in this revision.

There is one more set of figures, from Reuters, for Energy as a whole.

Energy operating profit was JPY 55.6bn in the second quarter, up sharply from JPY 1.2bn a year earlier.

Reuters gives the reasons for that increase as higher sales of automotive batteries in North America and higher sales of data-centre storage systems.

So it includes higher automotive battery sales, not only AI-related ones.

The AI-related numbers are certainly strong, but they cannot be lumped together as "AI raised the profit".

3. What the non-AI factors did, and by how much

Reuters lists the following factors behind the guidance raise.

Price changes, favourable FX, the disappearance of last year's US tariff impact, and higher AI-related sales in Industry: four in all.

Amounts can be followed only for the items that changed second-quarter adjusted operating profit.

Adjusted operating profit rose from JPY 90.4bn a year earlier to JPY 213.2bn.

The increase was JPY 122.8bn.

In the company's explanation as reported by Mynavi News, there were three positive items.

Price revisions and rationalisation came to JPY 74bn, volume growth to JPY 60bn, and fixed-cost improvement to JPY 21bn.

The largest item is not volume growth but price revisions and rationalisation.

Volume growth is profit earned by selling more. AI-related volume should be included there, but its breakdown is not disclosed.

So the effect of changing prices and cutting costs was larger than that volume growth.

Adding the three gives JPY 155bn, about JPY 32bn more than the actual increase of JPY 122.8bn.

The difference is thought to reflect items that worked in the negative direction.

Mynavi News separately cites a JPY 19bn recall cost for drum-type washer-dryers as a factor that pushed operating profit down.

Whether that recall cost is inside the gap, however, is not known from the materials available.

For FX and tariffs, no amounts were given.

Mynavi News writes that the revision "also reflects a review of the FX level and the refund effect of US tariffs".

Reuters cites, on tariffs, the disappearance of the US tariff impact that existed a year earlier.

The two articles describe the tariffs differently.

Whether last year's impact ended or paid tariffs are coming back cannot be decided from the materials available.

Either way, it is a factor unrelated to AI demand.

The company's presentation slides (PDF) could not be opened from here.

They may contain the FX and tariff amounts.

4. How splitting the factors changes the way you read the forecast

Whether each factor continues differs from one to the next.

FX: the tailwind weakens if the yen strengthens.

Panasonic's earnings release also lists excessive fluctuation of exchange rates between the yen and the dollar, euro, yuan and other currencies as one of its risks.

The effect of last year's tariff impact ending is an increase relative to the prior year, so the same amount does not appear again the following year.

Price revisions and rationalisation are areas the company can move itself.

AI demand is set by the market.

Kusumi said data-centre demand is expected to continue, while also mentioning that construction of data centres could be delayed by power supply and labour shortages.

He also indicated that demand as assumed does not necessarily turn into orders.

There is another point in this forecast that is hard to read.

Subtract the first-half actual of JPY 363.2bn from the JPY 710bn operating profit forecast, and the second half comes to JPY 346.8bn.

That is about JPY 16bn less than the first half.

AI demand is described as having expanded more than assumed, yet second-half profit is forecast to fall below the first half.

The reason was not written in the materials available.

Prior-year second-half operating profit was JPY 71.4bn, so this is about 4.9x the prior year.

Last year's low base is also part of why the profit growth rate looks so large.

Kusumi himself said that for the shift to a growth phase "signs are starting to appear", but that there are various risks and he could not say it unreservedly, taking a cautious stance.

Why it matters for global investors

Panasonic's fiscal year ends on March 31, so the current year here (FY2027/3) runs from April 2026 to March 2027, and the second quarter is July to September. The figures come from an interim earnings release prepared under IFRS, and adjusted operating profit is a reference figure the company presents alongside operating profit; the release does not explain the JPY 70bn gap between the two. Much of the disclosure, including the presentation slides referred to in this post, is published in Japanese, so the numbers are best checked against the primary documents. Annual securities reports and other statutory filings are published on EDINET, Japan's FSA filing system (the counterpart to SEC EDGAR).

Closing

The headline "guidance raised on AI demand" is best read split into price, FX, last year's tariff impact and AI-related volume.

Then separate the items whose amounts can be confirmed from those that cannot.

I hope this helps next time you open the company's presentation.
