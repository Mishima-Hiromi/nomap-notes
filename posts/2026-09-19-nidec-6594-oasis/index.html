Nidec (6594): Oasis lifts its stake to 7.97% and rewrites its purpose

A 1.19-point increase that carries far more than the number suggests

The stake went up by only 1.19 percentage points. If that is all you look at, the move looks small.

But read the amendment report Oasis Management filed, and a change appears that the size of the number cannot measure.

The stated purpose of holding was rewritten, and the additional shares were bought at a price above the fund's own average. Put those two together and the picture changes completely.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • Oasis Management raised its holding in Nidec Corporation (6594) from 6.78% to 7.97%. The increase is 1.19 percentage points.
  • At the same time, two things became clear: the purpose of holding was switched to "important proposal conduct", and the extra shares were bought at a price above the past average acquisition price.
  • Neither point can be explained by an opportunistic investment that trades around the share price.
  • Oasis appears to have entered a phase of deepening its engagement with Nidec regardless of price timing. The small size of the ratio change should not mislead you.

1. The world's largest motor maker, and an activist fund from Hong Kong

Nidec Corporation (6594)

Nidec Corporation (securities code 6594, formerly Nihon Densan) is the world's largest comprehensive motor manufacturer, making everything from precision small motors to the very large motors used in vehicles and industrial equipment.

Company website: https://www.nidec.com/

Oasis Management

Oasis Management is a Hong Kong-based activist fund founded in 2002 by Seth Fischer.

It also has an office in Tokyo.

Company website: https://oasiscm.com/

The accounting treatment questions at Nidec have already been reported on.

This post does not go into those details.

It reports only the facts of what Oasis did as a shareholder.

2. Why three filings carry the same reference date

Oasis Management (EDINET filer code E31883) has filed three large-shareholding reports and amendments concerning Nidec (issuer code E01975) most recently.

All three fix the same reference date, 30 September 2025.

The chronology therefore has to be read from the filing dates.

The first filing showed a holding ratio of 6.74%, on 80,322,406 shares.

Total acquisition funding was JPY 178.33bn, and the estimated acquisition price was JPY 2,220.2 per share.

The second showed a holding ratio of 6.78%, on 80,813,098 shares.

Total acquisition funding was JPY 179.41bn.

At that point the stated reason for change was "change of holding purpose" only.

The purpose category was "activist_implied".

The third is an amendment report filed on 16 September 2026.

The holding ratio is 7.97%, on 95,067,750 shares.

Total acquisition funding is JPY 216.54bn, and the estimated acquisition price is JPY 2,277.8 per share.

The reasons for change list three items: change of holding purpose, change of important proposal conduct, and an increase of 1% or more in the shareholding ratio.

The purpose category has switched to "activist".

Follow only the ratio, and the move from 6.78% to 7.97% is no more than a 1.19 percentage point increase.

But this document has two points that matter more than the change in the ratio.

3. An upgrade that 1.19 points cannot measure

Under Japan's large-shareholding disclosure system, the purpose-of-holding field carries more weight than the rise or fall of the ratio.

The second filing's reason for change was "change of holding purpose" only.

The purpose category was also "activist_implied", meaning it stayed at the level of suggesting activist-type behaviour.

The third amendment report states the reasons for change as follows.

Change of holding purpose, change of important proposal conduct, and an increase of 1% or more in the shareholding ratio

--- From Oasis Management's amendment report (filed 16 September 2026)

The purpose category has also switched to "activist".

That means the fund has shown, through a statutory disclosure, that it intends to make concrete proposals or demands to management.

This is why a change of only 1.19 percentage points can still be read as Oasis taking its engagement with Nidec one step further.

If Oasis were merely adjusting its position in line with market moves, it would have no reason to go to the trouble of changing its stated purpose.

4. Why the buying looks like buying at a high

This amendment report lists 20 transactions between 24 July and 9 September 2026.

The shares Oasis acquired during that period total 12,614,414.

The weighted average acquisition price over the period was JPY 2,632.5.

That is well above the average acquisition price so far, JPY 2,277.8.

In other words, Oasis is not adding to its position by picking up the downside.

It is building the position even at prices above what it has paid on average.

An investor waiting for a cheap entry would not make this move.

5. Why Oasis keeps buying while the accounting questions are in the news

An upgraded purpose of holding, and buying at prices above the average acquisition price.

Look at those as two separate events, and the small 1.19 percentage point increase pulls your reading down with it.

Read them together and the meaning changes.

Oasis is not choosing moments when the price is favourable. It has entered a phase of strengthening its engagement with Nidec regardless of price timing.

The investment has reached JPY 216.54bn, which makes it one of the largest activist positions in Japan.

The accounting treatment questions at Nidec have already been reported on.

Oasis's move here cannot be viewed separately from that earlier reporting.

The point to watch is not the small size of the ratio but the switch of purpose and the buying regardless of price.

Track only the ratio and you will miss the move.

An upgraded purpose, and buying above the average price. Those two facts say that Oasis's stance has already changed.

The next document it files will be worth watching.

Why it matters for global investors

Large-shareholding reports are Japan's version of the 5% beneficial-ownership filing familiar from the United States: once a holder crosses the threshold it must disclose the stake, and later changes come as amendment reports.

Most of these documents, like the rest of EDINET, are published in Japanese only, so the purpose-of-holding field rarely reaches an English-speaking audience.

That field is where a fund states whether it intends to make important proposals to management, which is why a switch from an implied activist stance to a declared one is a signal that a stake-size number cannot carry.

Oasis is a Hong Kong-based fund with a Tokyo office, so this is a foreign investor working inside a Japanese governance process.

Closing

A 1.19 percentage point increase is easy to scroll past.

What makes it worth reading is the purpose field and the price paid, not the size of the stake.

Disclosure documents are worth reading to the end, not just the headline number.
