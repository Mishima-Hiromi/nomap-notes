JINS HOLDINGS (3046 JP): revenue up 14.4%, operating profit down 5.3%

Gross profit grew; SG&A grew faster, and overseas stores covered part of the gap

Revenue rose 14%. You would expect profit to rise with it.

JINS HOLDINGS' results, published on October 9, went the other way.

Revenue crossed JPY 100bn for the first time, and operating profit came in below the prior year.

Here is the arithmetic, line by line.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from Japanese-language earnings releases to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • For the fiscal year ended August 2026, revenue rose 14.4% to JPY 111.2bn. Operating profit fell 5.3% to JPY 11.4bn.
  • The cause is not a weaker gross margin. Gross profit rose by JPY 11.4bn, and SG&A expenses rose by JPY 12.0bn, slightly more.
  • Domestic operating profit fell 18.6%; overseas operating profit roughly tripled and filled part of the hole.
  • Guidance for the year ending August 2027 pairs revenue growth of 11.7% with operating profit growth of 0.4%.

1. Revenue up, operating profit down: what is inside the result

What JINS HOLDINGS is

The company runs the eyewear brand JINS.

According to its earnings release, it uses a specialty-store-retailer (SPA) model, handling planning, production and sales in-house.

Company website: https://jinsholdings.com

Its shares carry securities code 3046.

The consolidated profit and loss statement

The table sets the two fiscal years side by side, as reported in the earnings release.

Revenue rose by JPY 14.0bn.

Gross profit rose by JPY 11.4bn as well.

The gross margin edged up from 78.0% to 78.4%.

So this is not a case of the sales mix breaking down and dragging profit with it.

SG&A expenses, meanwhile, rose by JPY 12.0bn.

The increase in SG&A was about JPY 0.6bn larger than the increase in gross profit, and that is the entire decline in operating profit.

SG&A as a share of revenue rose from 65.5% to 68.1%.

2. What the release lets you infer about the SG&A increase

The earnings release does not break the SG&A increase down by item.

The balance sheet and the cash flow statement, however, show that investment rose sharply.

Domestically, the company opened 55 stores and closed 4. It ended with 591 domestic stores.

It also opened JINS Ginza and a JINS Shinjuku store of about 1,000 sqm.

JINS Ginza, opened as our first global flagship store, and JINS Shinjuku, which carries the largest product lineup in about 1,000 sqm, have both performed above the original plan.

--- JINS HOLDINGS, FY8/2026 earnings release (translated from Japanese)

There is a change on the systems side as well.

Software on the balance sheet rose from JPY 2.4bn to JPY 8.2bn. Total intangible fixed assets went from JPY 5.7bn to JPY 10.3bn.

Depreciation in the cash flow statement was JPY 4.1bn, up JPY 1.2bn from JPY 2.9bn in the prior year (the prior-year figure comes from the annual securities report as recorded in EDINET DB).

That increase in depreciation is only about a tenth of the JPY 12.0bn rise in SG&A.

The rest is most naturally read as personnel and store operating costs that grow with new openings. The release does not itemise it.

What can be said is this: at a stage when the company is adding stores and investing in systems, the costs arrived before the profit did.

3. Domestic profit fell, and overseas filled the gap

Splitting operating profit by region changes the picture.

The domestic eyewear segment had revenue of JPY 84.7bn, up 10.5%.

Operating profit was JPY 9.2bn, down 18.6%.

Back-calculating the prior year from the change rate gives roughly JPY 11.3bn falling to JPY 9.2bn.

The operating margin on domestic revenue fell from about 14.8% to 10.9%.

The overseas eyewear segment had revenue of JPY 26.5bn, up 29.0%.

Operating profit was JPY 2.2bn, up 197.8%, or roughly 3x. The back-calculated prior year is about JPY 0.7bn.

Overseas profit rose from about 6.2% to 19.4% of consolidated operating profit.

The release says China is holding up well as the effects of a business-structure reform show, that new stores are performing well in the United States, and that Taiwan is steady.

Without the overseas growth, consolidated operating profit would have fallen much further.

4. Below the July forecast, and flat operating profit planned for next year

Measured against the company's own outlook, the landing was weak.

At the third-quarter results on July 10, the company forecast full-year operating profit of JPY 12.8bn (the earnings-release data recorded in EDINET DB).

The actual figure was JPY 11.5bn, about 10% below the forecast.

Net income also came in at JPY 7.8bn against a forecast of JPY 8.6bn.

For the year ending August 2027, the company forecasts revenue of JPY 124.3bn (up 11.7%), operating profit of JPY 11.5bn (up 0.4%) and net income of JPY 7.8bn (flat).

In margin terms, operating profit margin goes from 12.4% at the peak in FY8/2025, to 10.3% this year, to a forecast 9.3% next year.

For the first half, from September 2026 to February 2027, operating profit is forecast at JPY 3.9bn, down 21.7% year on year.

The annual dividend is JPY 104, down JPY 5 from JPY 109. Next year's forecast is also JPY 104, split into JPY 35 at the interim and JPY 69 at the year-end.

For the coming year the company says it will keep opening stores and investing in IT, while working to improve the gross margin through a pricing strategy with clear priorities.

Revenue growth is expected to continue. The point at which profit catches up is not in next year's forecast.

Why it matters for global investors

Japanese companies publish their earnings releases and annual securities reports in Japanese, and EDINET is the system where those filings live, the closest counterpart to SEC EDGAR. JINS HOLDINGS reports on an August fiscal year-end, so its first half runs from September to February, which is why the interim outlook above covers that window. The overseas eyewear segment discussed here includes China, the United States and Taiwan, so its profit line is the clearest read on whether the group's growth spending outside Japan is paying off. The shares trade on the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange under code 3046.

Closing

"Revenue growth" and "profit growth" are two different things.

These JINS results are a clean example of the difference.

Gross profit is still growing, so the product economics do not look broken.

What to watch is when the costs paid up front come back as profit.

The next earnings release is the place to check whether SG&A growth is closing in on revenue growth.
