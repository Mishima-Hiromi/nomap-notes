ROHM (6963): ordinary profit down 48.4%, yet the full-year forecast went up 47%

The raised numbers assume the second half roughly doubles its operating margin without selling more

ROHM raised its profit forecast sharply on November 5, 2026.

Ordinary profit guidance went from JPY 36.0bn to JPY 53.0bn. On the headline alone, it looks like a company with momentum.

Take the latest quarter, July to September, by itself, and ordinary profit is about 52% of the prior-year figure: roughly half.

This post checks what the raised numbers assume, using ROHM's own disclosures.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its own disclosures, from Japanese-language earnings releases to annual securities reports on EDINET, the regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Summary

  • ROHM raised its full-year operating profit forecast from JPY 30.0bn to JPY 50.0bn, and ordinary profit from JPY 36.0bn to JPY 53.0bn.
  • Ordinary profit in July-September was about JPY 5.6bn, down 48.4% from a year earlier. Operating profit was about JPY 7.9bn, slightly above the prior-year quarter.
  • The main reason ordinary profit halved was a non-operating foreign exchange loss of about JPY 4.1bn.
  • Delivering the full-year forecast requires raising the October-March operating margin from 5.5% in July-September to 12.0%, roughly double, and the company does not assume any increase in sales.

1. What the full-year revision changed

ROHM (securities code 6963) makes semiconductors.

Its earnings release divides the business into four segments: LSI, discrete semiconductor devices, modules and other.

Company website: https://www.rohm.com/

On November 5 it published a notice of revision to its earnings forecasts and raised its full-year outlook for the fiscal year ending March 2027.

Operating profit is the profit earned by the core business.

Ordinary profit is operating profit plus or minus interest, dividends and foreign exchange gains and losses.

The sales forecast went from JPY 510.0bn to JPY 550.0bn, and operating profit from JPY 30.0bn to JPY 50.0bn.

Ordinary profit went from JPY 36.0bn to JPY 53.0bn, a raise of 47.2%.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent went from JPY 29.0bn to JPY 45.0bn.

The prior year (fiscal year ended March 2026) came in at operating profit of JPY 10.9bn and a net loss of JPY 158.4bn.

For the reasons, the company cites the following points.

Investment in data centers is rising as generative AI spreads, and demand for servers and storage stays at a high level.

In industrial equipment, a recovery is visible, centered on factory automation (FA).

And even with raw material and logistics costs rising, price revisions and cost cuts are moving forward.

One sentence in the notice, explaining the revision, shows how the company sees profitability:

We expect profitability to improve beyond the assumption at the start of the fiscal year.

(ROHM, notice of revision to earnings forecasts, November 5, 2026)

What is worth noting here is that what the company cites is not only sales growth but also an improvement in profit margin.

The next section checks how far the latest quarter supports that improvement.

2. Why ordinary profit halved in July-September

ROHM's earnings release shows cumulative figures for April to September.

The April-June numbers are in the first-quarter earnings release from August, so subtracting gives July-September on its own.

Sales were JPY 143.7bn, up 12.3% from JPY 128.0bn a year earlier.

Operating profit was about JPY 7.9bn, 5.3% above the prior-year JPY 7.5bn.

The operating margin was 5.5%, a little below the prior-year 5.8%.

Ordinary profit, by contrast, was about JPY 5.6bn, down 48.4% from about JPY 10.9bn a year earlier.

Core profit was flat, yet ordinary profit halved.

The gap sits below the operating line.

Non-operating expenses for the first half include an FX loss of JPY 4.6bn.

The April-June FX loss was JPY 0.48bn, so about JPY 4.1bn of the loss was booked in July-September alone.

In the prior-year July-September quarter, the FX loss was only about JPY 0.05bn.

The earnings release does not say why the FX loss was this large in July-September.

So "a steep profit fall in July-September" refers to ordinary profit; operating profit from the core business actually rose slightly.

That operating profit also fell quarter on quarter, from about JPY 9.6bn in April-June to about JPY 7.9bn in July-September.

Sales rose while operating profit fell.

To check the full-year forecast, the next step is to pull out the second-half numbers.

3. The second-half plan doubles the margin without raising sales

Subtracting the first-half result from the full-year forecast gives what the company expects for October to March (the second half).

Sales are JPY 270.5bn.

That is about JPY 9.0bn less than the JPY 279.5bn first half; averaged per quarter it is JPY 135.3bn, almost the same as JPY 135.7bn in April-June.

It is below the JPY 143.7bn of July-September.

Operating profit is JPY 32.5bn.

The plan is to earn about 1.9x the JPY 17.5bn first half in the second half alone, when sales fall.

That is an operating margin of 12.0%.

Set against 5.5% in July-September and 6.3% for the first half as a whole, the second-half forecast is roughly double.

In the prior-year second half, sales were JPY 236.9bn and operating profit JPY 3.2bn, an operating margin of 1.4%.

This revision adds JPY 20.0bn to full-year operating profit.

The previous full-year forecast of JPY 30.0bn minus the JPY 17.5bn first half that is now known leaves JPY 12.5bn needed in the second half.

The revision can be read as lifting that second-half operating profit from JPY 12.5bn to JPY 32.5bn, an increase of JPY 20.0bn.

The earnings release and the revision notice I read do not break down how much of that JPY 20.0bn comes from price revisions, cost cuts, lower depreciation and so on.

The sales outlook does not assume growth from the first half.

A calculation in which profit doubles while sales are flat or lower is a forecast that builds in a large margin improvement in the second half. The company cites "price optimization and cost reductions" among the reasons for the revision.

The second-half exchange rate assumption is JPY 153 to the US dollar. July-September already produced an FX loss.

None of this means the forecast is bound to miss.

The earnings release also states that actual results may differ materially from the company's forecasts.

4. Reading the headline revision apart from the latest numbers

Putting the numbers so far together, the reading falls into three parts.

The first is the size of the full-year revision.

A raise of JPY 20.0bn in operating profit and JPY 17.0bn in ordinary profit is disclosed fact.

The second is the latest result.

July-September operating profit was 5.3% above a year earlier but down from April-June.

The 48.4% fall in ordinary profit needs to be read with a discount for the part caused by the FX loss.

The third is the assumption that supports the forecast.

Raising the operating margin to 12.0% without increasing sales is the number required to deliver the full-year forecast.

First-half net income also includes a JPY 7.46bn gain on the sale of investment securities.

The JPY 15.58bn first-half net income is not a figure earned by the core business alone.

So judging momentum from the headline "ordinary profit raised 47%" alone would miss both the weakness in the latest quarter and how much is being asked of the second half.

In the next quarterly results, how far the operating margin has risen from 5.5% in July-September will be the first evidence for judging whether the forecast holds.

Why it matters for global investors

ROHM reports under Japanese GAAP, and ordinary profit is a line between operating profit and net income that does not exist under IFRS or US GAAP: it adds interest, dividends and foreign exchange gains and losses to operating profit, which is why one quarter can show a small rise in operating profit and a halving of ordinary profit. ROHM's fiscal year runs from April to March, so the first half here is April to September and the second half October to March. The filings behind these figures are published in Japanese and are also available through EDINET, the Financial Services Agency's disclosure system, so the consolidated lines are worth checking against the primary documents.

Closing

When you see a headline about a raised forecast, check once which period's results are carrying the number.

For ROHM, the answer was the second half that has not been reported yet.

Whether the forecast catches up with actual results will show in the next set of earnings.
