Ichikawa (3513 JP): 64% of the net income upgrade is a tax accounting effect

Raising the interim net income forecast to JPY 1,180 million, read from the company's own disclosure

"The company raised its net income forecast to nearly three times the old figure." That sounds like the core business suddenly got much stronger.

Ichikawa's announcement looks a little different once you read the numbers inside it.

A large part of the increase comes from a tax accounting entry.

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

Key points

  • On October 30, 2026, Ichikawa (securities code 3513) raised its interim net income forecast for the fiscal year ending March 2027 from JPY 400 million to JPY 1,180 million (+JPY 780 million).
  • One reason is an additional deferred tax asset. The company expects to book about JPY 500 million as income tax adjustments (a gain). That is about 64% of the JPY 780 million increase.
  • The operating profit forecast was raised by JPY 220 million and the ordinary profit forecast by JPY 340 million, both smaller than the net income increase.
  • The full-year net income forecast was raised by JPY 600 million. If the JPY 500 million tax effect from the interim period stays in the full year, it is about 83% of that increase.

1. What did Ichikawa announce?

Ichikawa is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Standard Market and makes belts for papermaking, among other products. The company's release mentions increased production of papermaking belts.

On October 30, 2026, the company announced "Recognition of Deferred Tax Assets and Revision of Consolidated Earnings Forecasts."

It covers the interim period (April to September 2026, the second quarter of the fiscal year ending March 2027) and the full-year consolidated forecast.

Interim net income (profit attributable to owners of the parent) goes from JPY 400 million to JPY 1,180 million, an increase of 195.0%.

Interim earnings per share is forecast at JPY 279.30, up from JPY 95.07.

Revenue is revised up by 6.8%, operating profit by 36.7% and ordinary profit by 56.7%.

The increase in net income is far larger than the increase in any other profit line.

2. What is a deferred tax asset, and why does only net income swell?

A deferred tax asset is an expected reduction in future tax payments, recorded on the balance sheet as an asset.

For example, past losses, or timing differences between accounting expenses and tax expenses, can mean lower taxes in the future.

A company can record it once it judges that its future profits are predictable enough to make the benefit certain.

On the income statement, the amount appears as "income tax adjustments (gain)."

This item reduces the tax expense, so it lifts net income even if the core business has not changed at all.

The company says it recorded the additional asset after "careful consideration" of whether it can be recovered.

The amount is about JPY 500 million of income tax adjustments (gain) in the interim period.

Operating profit and ordinary profit are calculated before this item, so it does not affect them.

That is why only the net income increase is so large.

3. Of the JPY 780 million increase, JPY 500 million is the tax effect

The interim net income increase is JPY 780 million.

About JPY 500 million of it is the income tax adjustment (gain).

500 divided by 780 is about 64% (my calculation).

The company's disclosure does not break down the remaining JPY 280 million or so.

If you simply subtract the tax effect, the new interim net income forecast would be about JPY 680 million (1,180 minus 500, my calculation).

The prior-year actual was JPY 697 million.

This subtraction is only a rough guide.

Other changes in taxes are not disclosed, so JPY 680 million cannot be called exact.

Still, the level without the tax effect is roughly in line with the prior year.

The JPY 1,180 million figure looks like 1.7x the prior-year interim result (JPY 697 million).

Much of that apparent growth can be read as coming from tax accounting.

4. Did the core business numbers rise?

Yes, the core business numbers did rise from the previous forecast.

As the reason for the revision, the company cites its efforts to increase production of papermaking belts, which advanced sales expansion in overseas markets.

The operating profit forecast goes from JPY 600 million to JPY 820 million, an increase of JPY 220 million.

Prior-year interim operating profit was JPY 788 million, so the forecast is about 4% above it.

Revenue goes from JPY 7,300 million to JPY 7,800 million, above the prior-year JPY 7,197 million.

On the other hand, the new ordinary profit forecast is JPY 940 million.

That is almost the same as the prior-year JPY 942 million.

So operating profit is above the prior year, while ordinary profit is flat.

The disclosure does not explain the items between operating profit and ordinary profit (non-operating income and expenses).

In the previous forecast, operating profit and ordinary profit were both JPY 600 million.

Now they are JPY 820 million and JPY 940 million, a gap of JPY 120 million.

The reason is not written, so we cannot tell.

In short, the core business really did improve.

But it is not as strong as the JPY 1,180 million net income figure makes it look.

5. In the full-year forecast, the tax effect is an even bigger share

The full-year forecast (fiscal year ending March 2027) was revised on the same day.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent goes from JPY 900 million to JPY 1,500 million, an increase of JPY 600 million.

If the roughly JPY 500 million booked in the interim period remains in the full year, about 83% of the increase is the tax effect (my calculation).

Full-year operating profit rises only JPY 100 million, from JPY 1,300 million to JPY 1,400 million.

Results for the previous fiscal year (ended March 2026) were operating profit of JPY 1,570 million, ordinary profit of JPY 1,633 million and net income of JPY 1,173 million.

Set against that, the new full-year operating profit forecast of JPY 1,400 million is about 11% lower than the previous year.

Ordinary profit, at JPY 1,550 million, is also below the previous year.

Only net income, at JPY 1,500 million, is above the previous year.

So the full-year outlook has the core business below last year while net income alone exceeds it.

This mismatch can be read as largely due to the additional deferred tax asset (the company does not give a full-year breakdown).

Why it matters for global investors

Japanese earnings forecast revisions are published mostly in Japanese, and headline figures such as net income can move for accounting reasons unrelated to the core business.

When you see a large revision, check whether the net income increase is in proportion to the operating profit and ordinary profit increases.

If it is not, a tax or other accounting item may be driving the gap, as it is here.

Closing

When you see a forecast revision, first check whether the net income increase is in proportion to the operating profit and ordinary profit increases.

When it is not, accounting items such as taxes may be moving.

For Ichikawa this time, the revision combined an improvement in the core business with a tax effect.

In the next earnings report, look at the actual interim net income and at what is inside the income tax adjustments.
