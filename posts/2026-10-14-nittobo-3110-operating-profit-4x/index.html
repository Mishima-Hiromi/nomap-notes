Nitto Boseki (3110 JP): operating profit up 4.3x in three years

A Japanese glass-fiber maker, read straight from its EDINET filings

A company that makes its money from thread-like material grew operating profit 4.3x in three years.

Nitto Boseki Co., Ltd. (Nittobo, securities code 3110, listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime Market) makes glass-fiber yarn and cloth for electronic materials, composites that strengthen plastics, insulation materials, and in-vitro diagnostic reagents.

Company website: https://www.nittobo.co.jp/

About this series: each post takes one listed Japanese company and reads its disclosures on EDINET, the Japanese regulator's filing system (Japan's counterpart to SEC EDGAR), where most documents are published only in Japanese.

1. Operating profit: JPY 4.9bn to JPY 20.8bn

The number to watch is operating profit, the profit from the core business.

Operating profit fell to JPY 4.9bn in FY2023. In FY2026 it reached JPY 20.8bn. That is about 4.3x in three years.

Revenue rose from JPY 87.5bn to JPY 118.2bn over the same period, about 1.4x. Profit grew much faster than sales.

2. Net income: JPY 12.8bn to JPY 41.8bn, but with a one-off

Now look at the bottom line.

Net income in FY2026 was JPY 41.8bn, about 3.3x the JPY 12.8bn of the previous year.

Net income is twice operating profit (JPY 20.8bn) because the company booked JPY 38.1bn in extraordinary gains. Extraordinary gains are one-time items that occur only in that year.

Core profit and one-off profit need to be read separately.

As a result, return on equity (ROE, profit as a share of shareholders' equity) rose to 27.5% from 10.4% the year before. That figure includes the one-off gains.

Why it matters for global investors

Most Japanese filings, including the annual securities report (yukashoken hokokusho), are published on EDINET only in Japanese, so a number like this is easy to misread from outside Japan.

Two things in this case deserve care. First, the headline net income includes JPY 38.1bn of extraordinary gains, so it is not a clean measure of the business. Second, the latest earnings disclosure shows 188.62 million shares outstanding, against 37.72 million in the FY2026 annual securities report, roughly five times as many. When comparing per-share figures across years, check for changes in the share count.

Closing

When profit jumps in one year, it helps to split it into its parts.

Operating profit up 4.3x and net income up 3.3x are both "growth", but they mean different things.
